Dating websites · 18+

Dating website scams: the profile is on a site. The steal is usually off it.

A calm handbook for fraud that starts on a dating website or web profile — not a second types catalog, and not the swipe-app page. How website-side scams look, the email and WhatsApp rush, fake verification fees, and the first moves if cash already left.

Date Safely editors Updated Sep 13, 2026 18+

Quick answer

Dating website scams start on a desktop or web profile — Match, Plenty of Fish, eHarmony, OurTime, or a smaller site — then leave that inbox for email or WhatsApp before a gift-card, crypto, or “verification fee” ask. That is a different door from a Tinder swipe. Types map: online dating scams. App-swipe patterns: dating app scams. Romance hub: romance scams.

Key takeaways

  • The website profile is inventory. The steal is gift cards, wires, crypto, or a fake fee billed through email or chat.
  • Email or WhatsApp the same day is the hinge — “I left the site” is not a preference.
  • Nobody in a member thread can charge you to verify, unlock messages, or stay visible. Real site verification is free and on the official domain.
  • If cash left: stop, bank same day, report. A paid “recovery” is a second scam.

What dating-website scams are — and what this page is not

People search dating website scams after a Match, Plenty of Fish, or eHarmony chat turned expensive — or after a web profile they never met asked for a “quick” gift card in email. The phrase is narrower than romance fraud and different from a swipe-app match.

Romance fraud is the language the FTC and FBI use when someone pretends to be a romantic partner so they can get paid. That crime can start on a dating website, a phone app, Instagram, or a “wrong number” text. Hub: romance scams explained. The types catalog — romance, pig butchering, catfish money asks — lives on online dating scams. The swipe-app surface (Tinder, Bumble, Hinge, in-app chat, same-day WhatsApp) is dating app scams.

Dating website scams use the same payment rails, but the door is a web profile. Operators still prefer older desktop sites and smaller international boards because the inbox is email-heavy, paid messaging is normal, and many members are used to leaving the site to “talk properly.” This page stays on that surface: the site profile, the site inbox, the move to personal email or WhatsApp, and the asks that are written to sound like website problems (a locked profile, a message-unlock fee, a “support” verification).

A surprise Match or eHarmony membership renewal is a billing trap, not this page. Cancel the store or card subscription on the official site — start with how to cancel dating apps. Ordinary dating on these sites is still ordinary if you keep a few rules. Habits: is online dating safe.

What they look like on a dating website

The photos change. The site brand changes. The sequence does not. After a view or a paid message, watch for a profile that exists to leave the site’s logs and open a payment rail.

  • Polished web profile, thin life. Travel, military, oil, or “I work overseas” explains why they cannot video and why a fee appeared. Photos look like a catalog. The city in the bio does not match the story in the inbox.
  • They found you by email first. A message that looks like a site notification — “someone liked you,” “your profile is popular” — with a link that is not the official domain, or a reply-to address that is not the site. Clone sites exist to harvest passwords and cards. Type the real URL yourself.
  • Paid-message pressure. On sites that charge per note, they ask you to “save money” by moving to Gmail or WhatsApp after one compliment. The report button stays behind on purpose.
  • A second account posing as support. “Trust & Safety” in the site inbox or in email says your account will be hidden until you pay or upload ID. Real support does not bill gift cards and does not ask for a selfie next to your license in a member thread.

Stolen or generated photos are common on web profiles too. Photo checks: how to spot fake profiles. AI faces: AI dating scams in 2026. Walk-away lists: online dating red flags · romance scam red flags checklist.

The off-site rush (email, WhatsApp, Telegram)

Within hours — sometimes before a second reply on the site — they ask to move. “I left Match.” “The site keeps charging me to write.” “Email is easier.” “WhatsApp is more private.” Dating websites record reports and can close a crew’s accounts. A personal Gmail thread or WhatsApp chat does not. The report button stays on the site on purpose.

A real match can stay in the original site inbox until unscripted live video. If the move is the first urgency in the chat, treat it as the setup, not a preference. The same hinge on swipe apps is documented on dating app scams; on websites the first hop is often email, then WhatsApp. Wrong-number and romance-bait texts that skip the site entirely: scam text messages.

Money, crypto, and gift-card asks

Romance scammers do not open with “wire me everything.” The first request is written as a temporary favor between people who already care about each other. How that sentence usually sounds: romance scam scripts.

  • Gift cards “just this once” — iTunes, Steam, Target, Google Play. Irreversible on purpose. Not how rent, medicine, or a visa is paid. The first card is a test of whether the rail works.
  • Wires, Zelle, Cash App, Western Union — a hospital deposit, a ticket “so I can come see you,” customs on a package. Amount is not the test. Someone you have not met should not be on your payment apps.
  • Crypto or a trading screenshot — they stayed warm on the site, moved to Telegram, then “a friend” showed them a platform. A small withdrawal that “works” is the hook. That long version is pig butchering (dating-app arc: pig butchering on dating apps).
  • Military or overseas work — deployed soldier, contractor, oil rig. Leave papers, equipment, a held package. Real US service members are not billed through a dating-site inbox. Costume: military romance scams.

You do not owe them a debate about the hospital or the chart. One strong money ask from someone you have not met is enough to stop.

Fake verification fees

This one is written to sound like a website problem. A match — or a second account posing as site support — says your profile will be hidden, their messages will vanish, or you must pay to unlock the thread. Sometimes they want gift cards. Sometimes they want a card number on a lookalike “verify” page. Sometimes they want a driver’s license and a selfie “so the site knows you’re real.”

Real Match, Plenty of Fish, eHarmony, and similar sites verify for free inside the official account settings — not in a member chat, not in a forwarded email, and not over WhatsApp. A request for your ID “so I know you’re real” is identity theft, not romance. Nobody can bill you a second time to stay visible. If the site itself charged a membership you did not want, that is a cancel and refund problem, not a verification fee from a stranger: cancel guides.

Identity and video refusal

Live, unscripted video on a tool you started — not a clip they send, not a “broken camera,” not a face that never turns to the side — is the identity check. Website scammers refuse it because the photos are stolen or generated. They offer a voice note, a gym clip, or a future visit that never lands. How to treat live versus recorded: video chat safety.

They may also refuse any identity that can be checked: a full name that reverse-searches, a job that exists, a city they can video from in daylight. Story details slip (age, the visit, the branch of service) while the money pitch stays smooth. That is data. A catfish who never asks for cash is a different problem; the money version is catfishing scam (definition: what is catfishing).

You do not owe them a debate about the site.

Real people who want a relationship can handle “I stay in the site inbox until live video” and “I do not send money, gift cards, or copies of my ID to people I have not met.” If that boundary ends the chat, the chat needed to end.

Stop, bank, report

Stop sending

No “one last” gift card. No unlock fee. No tax to release funds. No photo of your ID “just to verify.” More money does not buy the earlier money back. Do not send one last message to “catch them.” They already know.

Call the bank or card same day

Say dating website scam, romance scam, or romance fraud — the wording helps the fraud team. Flag wires, Zelle, gift-card purchase points, and any crypto on-ramp. Speed matters more than perfect wording.

Report the profile and file with authorities

Block and report on the dating website so other people stop opening that profile. US: ReportFraud.ftc.gov and IC3.gov. UK: Action Fraud. Australia: Scamwatch / ReportCyber. Step-by-step: how to report a dating scam.

Save evidence, tell someone you trust

Screenshots of the web profile, site inbox, email headers, wallet addresses, URLs, and receipts — before you block if you can. Shame is part of the design; a trusted friend reduces the chance you send again under a new “verification” story.

Government reports rarely cut a check. They still matter. Clusters of the same wallet, clone domain, or “I left Match” line are how cases get built. File even if the amount feels small.

Do not pay anyone who offers to get the money back.

After a dating-website loss, a second crew often arrives — a “lawyer,” crypto tracer, or fake FTC agent who wants a retainer, tax, or unlock fee. That is a recovery scam, sometimes run by the same operators. Legitimate agencies never charge for a refund. Anyone who does is a scammer. FAQ

What are dating website scams versus dating app scams?

Dating website scams start on a web profile (Match, Plenty of Fish, eHarmony, and smaller sites) and usually leave that inbox for email or WhatsApp. Dating app scams start after a swipe. Same payment rails; different door. Types map: online dating scams. App page: dating app scams. Romance hub: romance scams.

Why do they want email or WhatsApp so fast?

The report button stays on the dating website. Off-site chat is harder to flag and easier to run at scale. Stay in the original inbox until live video. If they push, that is data. Walk-away list: dating red flags.

What is a fake verification fee on a dating website?

A match or fake “support” account asks you to pay, send gift cards, or upload ID to unlock messages or stay visible. Real site verification is free and on the official domain. Nobody in a chat can bill you a second membership to stay listed.

What should I do if I already sent money?

Stop. Do not pay a release or second verification fee. Call the bank or card the same day, report the profile on the site, then file FTC ReportFraud and IC3 in the US (Action Fraud UK, Scamwatch AU). Checklist: report a dating scam. Do not pay a recovery firm — that warning is on get money back from a scam.
