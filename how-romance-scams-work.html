How romance scams work — the stages from bait profile to the first ask

A stage-by-stage explainer of the funnel — not a definition page, not a spotting checklist. How the chat is built, why they leave the app, how the first fee arrives, and where the pipeline forks into crisis rails or pig butchering.

Date Safely editors Updated Sep 13, 2026 18+

Quick answer

Romance scams work as a funnel, not a single lie. A bait profile starts the chat. Daily warmth — love bombing, or a slower version of the same — builds trust. They move you to WhatsApp or Telegram so the report button stays behind. Then a small, urgent fee: hospital, ticket, customs, “prove you care.” If that rail works they escalate — gift cards, Western Union, crypto, lottery tax, pet kennel, military leave — or fork into a fake trading site. That second path is pig butchering. Break the funnel before money leaves. If cash already left: bank the same day, then FTC ReportFraud and IC3. Definition: what is a romance scam. Spotting moves: how to spot a romance scammer.

Key takeaways

  • The romance is inventory. The product is an irreversible rail — gift cards, wires, crypto, or a fake platform.
  • Each stage exists to make the next one feel normal. You do not need every stage. Plenty of people send on the first ask.
  • Escalation costumes (customs, lottery, pet, military) and pig butchering are forks after the rail works — not a new crime.
  • Best break: stay on the app, refuse money to someone you have not met. If cash left: stop, bank, FTC, IC3. Anyone who later charges to recover funds is the second scam.

The funnel, not a single story

People type how do romance scams work, romance scam stages, and romance scam process when a chat suddenly feels like a pipeline — or after the first fee already left. Those are aliases for this page. Same crime. The useful picture is a funnel: each step is built to make the next one feel like care, not an invoice.

The definition page answers what it is: a fake relationship used to take money. The spotting page answers what to do with what you see. This page is the mechanism — how operators move a stranger from a dating-app grid to an irreversible rail. The FTC and FBI describe the same boring sequence: contact, private chat, then gift cards, wires, crypto, or a site they control.

You do not sit through a quiz. One completed stage is enough to leave. Grammar got better. AI faces got smoother. The order of the funnel did not change. Shame is part of the design. People wait. Waiting is how a $200 gift card becomes a five-figure wire.

Stage 1 — Bait profile

The first product is a face that can start a reply. Photos are stolen or generated. The bio pays the distance bill in advance: oil rig, deployed, overseas trading, widowed contractor, “just transferred.” That job is not flavor. It is why they can never meet, and later why a fee appears.

  • Inventory, not a life. Studio light, hotel balconies, a uniform that never wrinkles. A trail older than the match is the test — reverse-search more than one photo. Photo-first page: how to spot fake profiles.
  • A new account with a finished story. Odd hours that always match the job. A link to “verify” on another site. Type official URLs yourself. Stay in-app.
  • Contact that is not a match. Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, or a “wrong number” text. Same funnel. The dating app is only one door.

Tinder or Hinge photo verification means a face matched a selfie check at some point. It does not mean the person in the chat is that face. Useful. Not a background check. What a romance scam is, in one paragraph: what is a romance scam.

Stage 2 — Love bombing and trust

The second product is attachment. Daily check-ins. They remember the dog’s name. Soulmate language before shared reality. Love-bombing in a week still happens; slower crews now wait because everyone has read that tell. Warmth is inventory either way. It is what makes the later invoice feel like a favor between people who already care.

  • Volume is not intimacy. Long nights, voice notes, “good morning” clips. Scripted presence is cheap. Repeat, unscripted live video is not.
  • A future that is always next month. The visit, the flight, the leave. The calendar is bait. The deadline will be tonight, when money appears.
  • Isolation starts here. “Don’t tell your friends — they won’t understand us.” Secrecy protects the rail, not the relationship. A second adult knowing the name makes the next script harder.

This stage can last days or months. Length is not proof. A slow burn is still a funnel. Spotting moves while the thread is still open: how to spot a romance scammer.

Stage 3 — Off-app move

The first product in many romance cons is leaving the inbox that can still report them. WhatsApp, Telegram, Signal, or iMessage — “the app keeps logging me out,” “I prefer private,” “too many fake people in here.” Dating apps keep a report trail. Private apps do not.

  • They need the report button behind you before a five-minute live call you start. If they punish you for saying no, that is data. Real people who want a relationship can wait on Tinder, Bumble, or Hinge.
  • A new number every few days is a burned account being replaced. Volume across apps before you have met is a second channel, not closeness.
  • The money ask is easier off-app. Gift-card codes, wallet addresses, and “verify” links do not belong in a match thread. That is why they leave. Channel: WhatsApp dating scams.

If they need to move off-app before live video, that is already a completed stage. You do not owe them a preference debate.

Stage 4 — First fee or emergency ask

Romance scammers do not open with “wire me everything.” The first request is written to sound like a temporary favor. Small. Urgent. Emotional. Hospital deposit, stranded ticket, customs hold, “prove you care,” a $40 gift card “just this once.” Amount is not the test. It is a test of whether the rail works.

  • Medical or accident crisis — hospital, medicine, a child’s emergency. Urgency plus guilt.
  • Travel, visa, or a visit that suddenly costs money — they were “coming to see you” until a fee appeared.
  • “Verify you’re real” with a card, a code, or a link. Nobody who is who they say they are bills you to prove they are real. Dating apps do not collect card numbers in a match thread.

How to hear the first ask while the chat is still open: romance scam scripts. You do not owe them a debate about the story. A real partner can handle “I do not send money to people I have not met.” If that boundary ends the chat, the chat needed to end.

Stage 5 — Escalation rails

If the first fee posts, crews reuse the pipe. The costume rotates. The rail stays irreversible. Gift cards, Western Union or MoneyGram, a wallet, Zelle, Cash App. Each refusal becomes a new emergency. “I already paid my half — just cover the last hold” is a product line, not a status update.

  • Gift cards — iTunes, Google Play, Target, Steam. Codes are cash. Not how real people pay rent, customs, or a hospital. Rail: gift card scams.
  • Western Union, MoneyGram, bank wires — cash pickup and account-to-account sends that are hard to claw back. “The clerk said it has to be your name.” Rail: Western Union / wire scams.
  • Crypto — a “small test,” then a larger deposit or an unlock fee. Sometimes this is still a crisis costume. Sometimes it is the start of the pig-butchering fork below. Dating-app crypto path: crypto romance scams.
  • Customs — a package, a gift, documents “held at the airport.” There is no crate. Costume: customs-fee scams.
  • Lottery, inheritance, insurance — a prize or estate that needs a tax or release fee. Real prizes are free. Costume: lottery / inheritance romance scams.
  • Pet or puppy — stranded dog, vet bill, kennel release, customs on a crate. There is no animal. Costume: pet / puppy romance scams.
  • Military, oil, overseas contract — the job from Stage 1 invoices leave papers, travel, or equipment. US service members and real contractors do not fund official process through a civilian’s iTunes cards. Costume: military romance scams · oil-rig romance scams.

Ignore the letterhead. Listen for the rail. A PDF from a “customs desk” or a “lottery claims office” is inventory. Governments, lotteries, shelters, and real commands do not collect through a dating chat. Tick-box inventory of flags: romance scam red flags checklist.

Stage 6 — Optional pig-butchering fork

Not every crew keeps inventing hospitals. Some spend more weeks on warmth, then introduce charts. A “friend’s platform,” an invite-only link you did not type yourself, a small withdrawal that actually arrives. That success is engineered. Larger deposits follow. Then a tax, a KYC unlock fee, or a frozen balance.

That fork is pig butchering — a Mandarin phrase for fattening trust before the steal. Related to ordinary romance fraud, not identical. A simple romance scam often wants gift cards or a wire for a crisis. Pig butchering wants large deposits into a site the crew controls. Dating-app angle: pig butchering on dating apps.

The fork can appear instead of Stage 5, or after a small crisis fee already worked. Either way the first rule is the same: never send money to someone you met online. A trading screenshot is not a hot tip. It is the next invoice.

Quoted stage tells

Composites of lines victims quote to banks and to the FTC. You do not need a word-for-word match. The useful part is which stage just completed — and that the next invoice is already being written.

Bait profile / job story

“I’m a contractor on a rig / deployed until March. The hours are brutal so I live on this app. Once this contract ends I’m coming to your city. Don’t Google the company, HR gets weird about that.”

Why it works: the job explains no meet and, later, a fee. The tell: a life that cannot be checked, offered in the first hour. Reverse-search the photos. You start the call. Identity check: how to verify someone online.

Love bombing

“I have never felt this with anyone. You’re my person. I already told my sister about you. When I land we start our life — please don’t let your friends talk you out of us. They haven’t felt this.”

Why it works: you are written in as family before you have shared a room. The tell: isolation plus a future that is always next month. Warmth is the stage. It is not proof.

Off-app rush

“This app keeps logging me out. Add me on WhatsApp so we don’t lose each other — I already miss you. I don’t like leaving things in here; too many fake people.”

Why it works: it sounds like care, plus a shared enemy (the app). The tell: they need the report button behind you before live video. Stay on the app. If they leave, that is the stage completing. Channel: WhatsApp dating scams.

First fee

“Just this once — $80 on a Google Play / Target card for the hospital deposit / the ticket / to verify you’re real. I’ll pay you back when I land Friday. If you loved me you’d do this small thing. Don’t tell your sister.”

Why it works: it attacks your self-image, and the number looks temporary. The tell: a small card is not a character test. It is a test of whether the rail works. First-ask catalog: romance scam scripts.

Escalation costume

“The package / puppy / lottery check / leave papers are held — $380 to release. I already paid my half. Western Union or USDT tonight or they send it back. The clerk said a civilian has to cover it from your side.”

Why it works: bureaucracy feels grown-up, and you are “finishing” something. The tell: costume is interchangeable. Customs, lottery, pet, military — same invoice. Depth: customs · lottery · pet · military.

Pig-butchering fork

“I don’t usually share this. My mentor put me on a private board — look at this withdrawal. Start with $300 so you see it work. Then we scale together. Don’t tell your bank, they won’t understand the opportunity.”

Why it works: a small win that actually arrives. The tell: a platform you did not type yourself, plus secrecy. That is pig butchering, not a relationship project.

Where to break the funnel — before money leaves

The cheapest break is the earliest one you still have. You do not need to finish the pipeline to be sure. Order matters more than a perfect write-up.

  • At the profile. Reverse-search more than one photo. A stolen face or a job that exists only to explain distance is enough to pause. Next move is live video you start — not a second compliment.
  • At the off-app push. Stay on the dating app until you have done an unscripted call you placed. If they need WhatsApp first, the stage is already done. Leave.
  • At the first money ask. Any cash, gift card, wire, Zelle, or crypto from someone you have not met is the end of the funnel. Amount is not the test. Put the card back. Do not “meet halfway.”
  • After a first send, before the second. The next costume is already written. Stopping here is how a $200 test does not become a wire. More money does not buy the earlier money back.
  • At the pig fork. A platform you did not find yourself, a withdrawal that “proves” it, an unlock fee — walk away. Do not deposit to inspect the dashboard.

Walk-away list: dating scam red flags. Tick-box inventory: romance scam red flags checklist. You do not have to disprove the hospital, the kennel, or the lottery.

If money already left — stop, bank, FTC ReportFraud, IC3

Order matters more than a perfect write-up. If you are still holding a gift card, put it back. If a transfer is pending, call the bank before you reread the thread.

Stop sending

No last gift card. No smaller “compromise.” No unlock fee. No tax to release funds. No “one last” WhatsApp to catch them. They already know. More money does not buy the earlier money back.

Call the bank or card same day

Use the number on the back of the card — not a number from the chat. Say romance scam or romance fraud. Flag wires, Zelle, gift-card purchase points, Western Union pickup, and any crypto on-ramp. Speed matters more than perfect wording.

Report the profile, then FTC ReportFraud and IC3

Block and report on the app or social site so other people stop matching them. US: ReportFraud.ftc.gov and IC3.gov. UK: Action Fraud. Australia: Scamwatch / ReportCyber. Those filings compile scripts and wallets; they are not a refund desk. Checklist: how to report a dating scam.

Save evidence, tell someone you trust

Screenshots of the profile, chat, wallet addresses, URLs, and receipts — before you block if you can. Shame is why people wait and send again. One trusted person reduces the chance a second “release” script lands. You were targeted. That is not the same as being foolish.

Recovery-scam warning

After you map the funnel — or after money leaves — a second crew often arrives. Text, email, or a site that ranks for “romance scam recovery.” They pose as lawyers, platform investigators, government agents, or “victim advocates.” They say the wire is frozen, the gift cards can be refunded, or they found the operator. They need a retainer, an unlock fee, or your bank login.

The FTC’s line is short: do not trust someone who contacts you claiming they can recover lost money for a fee. Government agencies and legitimate organizations will not ask for money, gift cards, or crypto in exchange for a refund. Anyone who does is a scammer. Sometimes it is the same operators in a new costume.

  • They found you. You did not look up the number yourself.
  • They want payment first — retainer, unlock, tax, “AML clearance,” more gift cards or USDT.
  • They want remote access or your bank / exchange / wallet login.
  • They guarantee a percentage back if you act today.
  • They tell you not to talk to family or the bank because it would “tip off” the first scammer or “void the claim.”

There is no private firm that can reverse a PIN or a transfer you already signed, and no official who needs iTunes cards to process a romance-fraud report. If a recovery pitch already took more money, treat that as a new scam: stop, call the bank again, file another ReportFraud and IC3 report. Do not hire a third person to fix the second. What happens after the first money ask?
