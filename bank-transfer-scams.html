Bank transfer scams: when a dating match wants a wire or ACH to their account

A calm handbook for romance and dating chats that ask you to send a bank transfer — a domestic wire, an ACH credit, or “send it to my account and I’ll refund you” — after a love-bomb and an off-app rush.

Date Safely editors Updated Sep 13, 2026 18+

Quick answer

A bank transfer romance scam is a fake relationship (or a sudden crisis) built to get you to send money from your bank to an account they name. The label changes — ACH, domestic wire, “pay a person,” routing and account number, “I’ll refund you Friday.” The rail does not. Once the receiving bank pays it out, it behaves like cash. Stop sending. Screenshot the chat, the confirmation, and the receiving name. Call your bank’s fraud line the same day, then file FTC ReportFraud and IC3. Anyone who offers to get the wire back for a fee is running the second scam. Cash-pickup sister: Western Union / MoneyGram. P2P sister: Zelle / Cash App / Venmo. Recovery limits: get money back from a scam.

Key takeaways

  • Real partners, hospitals, customs offices, the military, and brokers do not collect a personal wire or ACH from a dating chat.
  • “Send to my account and I’ll refund you” is the steal, not a favor. A name that does not match the photos is the tell.
  • Same-day fraud-desk call + screenshots beats a perfect write-up next week. Wires and posted ACH are hard to reverse.
  • Anyone who offers to recover the bank transfer for a fee is running the second scam.

What a bank-transfer romance ask is

A bank transfer scam in a dating or romance context is simple: someone you have not met in person — often after warmth on Tinder, Bumble, Hinge, Instagram, or a “wrong number” text — asks you to send money from your bank to an account they control (or a mule’s). Searches for bank transfer scams, bank transfer romance scam, wire transfer dating scam, ACH romance scam, and wire fraud romance are aliases for this page, not five different cons.

The story may be a hospital deposit, a ticket so they can visit, a customs hold, a lockout, an “investment desk that only takes a wire,” or the line that does the most damage: “Send it to my account and I’ll refund you Friday.” The FTC’s public line is blunt: do not wire money or send a cash-like transfer to someone you have not met in person. Government agencies, banks, hospitals, and legitimate companies will not demand you pay a dating match that way. Neither will a real romantic partner who respects you. Broader map: romance scams · online dating scams · dating scam red flags.

This handbook is the account-to-account rail — routing numbers, ACH credits, domestic wires you start in your bank app or at a branch. Cash pickup at an agent is the sister page: Western Union / MoneyGram / Ria. Email-or-phone P2P is Zelle, Cash App, and Venmo. Friends & Family is PayPal scams. A check that looks real, then a demand to send the extra out, is fake-check scams.

The dollar amount is not the test. A $180 “verify the account” and a $6,000 “last ticket home” are the same crime. You do not owe them a debate about why a same-day wire is “the only thing that posts tonight.”

Early tells

The ask is late on purpose. Early suspicion would end the chat. The usual order — and the flags that show up before the routing number — are boring and reliable:

  • Love-bomb, then off-app. Fast warmth. They remember the dog’s name. Then WhatsApp, Telegram, iMessage, or email because “the app keeps logging me out.” The report button stays behind. Same rush, other costumes: online dating scams.
  • No live video you start — or video only after you send. A polished FaceTime that looks slightly off still is not proof. Photo checks: how to spot fake profiles.
  • A crisis that only a bank rail can fix tonight. Hospital, airport, customs, landlord, lawyer, “payroll hasn’t hit.” Cards and PayPal Goods and Services are “too slow” or “will flag the account.”
  • Routing and account in the chat — a screenshot, a PDF “invoice,” or a note that says “send to my account, I’ll refund you.” The beneficiary name often does not match the dating profile. They say it is a cousin, a business account, the hospital, or “my CO.”
  • Isolation. “Don’t tell your bank it’s for someone you met online.” “Don’t tell your sister.” That instruction protects the rail, not your privacy.
  • They want your account number too. “So I can refund you,” “so the hospital can send a receipt,” or “to verify.” Giving a stranger your routing and account number is how unauthorized debits and fake-check deposits start. You do not need to share it for a real relationship.

You do not need all six. Off-app + no live video + a bank-account story is enough. Walk-away list: dating scam red flags.

Why scammers prefer bank rails

Romance crews are not loyal to a brand. They are loyal to finality and to a story that sounds like grown-up finance. Bank transfers win on both.

  • You click Send — the bank calls that authorized. A merchant card dispute exists because a network sits between you and a business. A person-to-person wire or ACH you start from your own login usually does not get that button. Fraud desks often say “you authorized it.” Say romance scam or romance fraud, not “I paid a friend.”
  • The receiving account can empty the same day. A domestic wire can post in hours. Same-day ACH exists. A mule (or a stolen account) can move the money again before you have finished the chat. There is no hotel to charge back.
  • It looks more “real” than a gift card. A routing number and a confirmation PDF feel like paying a hospital or a broker. That costume is why people send larger amounts than they would as Amazon codes. Gift-card version of the same steal: gift card scams.
  • Limits are higher than Zelle. When a payment-app cap blocks the ask, they upgrade you to a branch wire or an ACH “to my account.” P2P handbook: Zelle / Cash App / Venmo.
  • Name matching is weak. Many banks pay to the account number, not to the name you typed. “Hospital billing” or a slightly misspelled legal name still lands in a mule account.
  • Shame buys the second send. Waiting is how a $300 “deposit” becomes a correction wire you cannot recall. Isolation (“don’t tell the banker the dating story”) is how that wait happens.

The bank logo is not buyer protection.

A credit-union app, a megabank wire desk, and an “online transfer to a person” are still you sending cash-like value to a stranger. “Only a wire works tonight” is pressure, not logistics. Isolation protects the rail.

ACH, domestic wire, and “send to my account and I’ll refund”

People type bank transfer for three different buttons. Crews use all three. The story changes. The idea does not: value leaves your bank and becomes someone else’s to spend.

  • Domestic wire (Fedwire / “bank wire”). Same-day, often within hours. You (or a teller) send to a receiving bank, account number, and beneficiary name. Once the receiving bank has paid it out, a recall is a request — not a right. There is no Visa/Mastercard chargeback path on a person-to-person wire.
  • ACH credit (“transfer,” “send to a bank account,” “pay a person”). Overnight or same-day, using a routing (ABA) number and account number. An ACH romance scam is the same invoice as a wire with a slower costume. If it is still pending, your bank can sometimes stop it. After it settles, returns are limited and often fail when you clicked Send yourself.
  • “Send to my account and I’ll refund you.” The relationship pitch. They need a deposit, a ticket, a hold, or “proof you’re serious.” They promise Friday payroll, a visit, or a screenshot of a refund that never posts in your app. The refund is the bait. The outbound transfer is the steal.
  • International wire / SWIFT. Same finality plus correspondent banks. “The hospital in [country] only takes a wire” is still a person-to-person send. Cash-pickup costume of that ask: Western Union scams.

If they sent you a check or an incoming ACH first and now want the extra wired out, stop. That inbound item is often stolen or will bounce. Detail: fake-check scams. PayPal costume of the same reverse-send: PayPal scams.

Quoted scripts

You do not need a word-for-word match. Grammar got better. The bank rail did not. These are the shapes victims keep quoting — hospital, “I’ll refund you,” a fake inbound, an investment wire, and a travel hold.

Hospital / “wire the deposit to this account”

“Baby I’m at the ER and they need a deposit before they treat me. Card won’t go through. Wire $1,100 to this routing and account — it’s the hospital billing office. I’ll refund you Friday when payroll hits. Please don’t tell anyone. I’m so embarrassed.”

Why it works: medical urgency plus shame plus numbers that look like a real payable. The tell: hospitals do not collect a personal ACH or wire from a dating match. Real emergencies go through insurance, a person who can see you, or a card — not a WhatsApp handle and a “billing” account that does not match the hospital’s published pay-to name. A lockout, a lawyer, or a broken-down rental is the same script with a new costume.

“Send it to my account — I’ll refund you”

“I just need you to send $700 to my account so I can book the ticket tonight. ACH is fine if a wire is too much trouble. I’ll refund you the minute I land. You’re the only person I trust. Don’t tell your family; they won’t understand us yet.”

Why it works: the visit is the prize; the transfer is framed as a loan between partners. The tell: a real person who wants a relationship can handle “I do not send bank transfers to people I have not met.” If that boundary ends the chat, the chat needed to end. Broader pattern: romance scams.

Fake inbound / overpayment, then a reverse wire

“I sent $3,400 by mistake — I meant $340 for the ticket. Wire $3,060 back to this account today and keep the rest. The ACH already cleared on my side. Screenshot attached.”

Why it works: you feel you are holding their money. The tell: the inbound payment is often stolen, a fake screenshot, or a check that will bounce. If you send a wire “back,” you eat the loss when the first item disappears. Do not refund anyone you met online. Open your bank app yourself — not a link from the chat. PayPal costume: PayPal scams.

Investment “desk” that only takes a bank wire

“My broker opened the account in both our names. The desk only takes a bank wire — not Zelle, not a card. $2,800 today and you’ll see it in the portal tonight. I’ll walk you through the login after you send the reference number.”

Why it works: it sounds like a grown-up financial step, not a gift. The tell: a real brokerage does not collect a personal wire from a dating-app match, and it does not need you to bypass card rails. A portal you did not type yourself is inventory. Crypto and fake-trading version of that steal: pig butchering on dating apps.

Travel / customs — “the agent is closed, use your bank”

“Customs is holding my kit and Western Union is closed. Same-day wire to this account and I board. $520. I’ll refund the duty when I land. Don’t tell your bank it’s for travel; they’ll delay it.”

Why it works: the visit plus a reason to skip the cash-pickup brand you already doubted. The tell: customs offices do not collect a personal wire from a stranger on WhatsApp. “Don’t tell your bank” is the whole con. Agent-window costume: Western Union scams. Package costume: customs-fee scams.

You do not owe them a debate about routing numbers.

Real people who want a relationship can handle “I do not send ACH, wires, or bank transfers to people I have not met” and “I stay on the app until live video.” If that boundary ends the chat, the chat needed to end.

Why these rails are hard to reverse

Credit-card merchant disputes exist because a card network sits between you and a business. Most person-to-person bank transfers do not work that way. Romance crews pick the rail with no chargeback button.

  • It is treated like cash you chose to send. You signed a wire form or tapped Send in the bank app. Fraud desks often label that “authorized.” The wording that helps is romance scam or romance fraud — not “I helped my partner get home.”
  • A wire has a short recall window. Once the receiving bank has paid it out, the sending bank usually cannot claw it back. Same-day is the whole strategy. International wires add correspondent banks and even less leverage.
  • Posted ACH is not a gift-receipt. If the credit has settled, a return is a long shot when you originated it. Pending is the hour that still matters. Call before you reread the thread.
  • The beneficiary is a mule, not a merchant. “Hospital,” “broker,” “cousin,” and a slightly misspelled legal name are how the money leaves the receiving bank. You cannot dispute a store the way you dispute a charged hotel.
  • Shame keeps people quiet long enough for the trail to go cold. Waiting is how a $200 “deposit” becomes a second wire you cannot recall.

Still call the linked bank or credit union the same day. Ask them to cancel a pending transfer or start a wire recall / ACH return. Zelle, PayPal Friends & Family, gift-card codes, and crypto have the same finality once they leave. Compare rails: how to get money back from a scam. Evidence to keep

Do not delete the thread to “move on.” The fraud desk, FTC ReportFraud, and IC3 all work from what you can paste. Save copies off the phone they still have access to if the match ever had your logins.

  • The chat — dating-app thread and the off-app app (WhatsApp, Telegram, iMessage, email). Full-screen screenshots, not cropped quotes.
  • Profile proof — URLs or handles, display name, photos, the phone or email they used after the move.
  • Bank confirmation — amount, date, time, status (pending / posted). Export the PDF if the app offers one.
  • Rail identifiers — wire IMAD/OMAD or federal reference; ACH trace number; memo line; confirmation number the teller wrote down.
  • Receiving side — bank name, routing (ABA) last four, account last four, beneficiary name they gave you, any “hospital” or “broker” label. Do not post the full account number in a public report field if a last-four plus bank name will do; keep the full number in your private folder for the bank.
  • What funded it — last four of the account or card, branch location if you walked in, the employee’s name if you have it.
  • Their inbound bait — fake “you’ve been paid” screenshots, check photos, emails with wire instructions. Do not open new links from those emails.

That packet is also what a recovery caller will ask you to hand over. Share it with your bank and with ReportFraud / IC3 — not with a stranger who found you. Reporting checklist: how to report a dating scam.

What to do the same day

Order matters more than a perfect write-up. If a transfer is still pending, call the bank before you reread the thread.

Stop sending

No “one last” wire. No second account because the first “didn’t clear.” No correction ACH. No refund of an “overpayment.” More money does not buy the earlier money back, and it does not make a real partner appear. They already know.

Screenshot everything

Chat (dating app and off-app), profile URLs or handles, confirmation screens, receiving name, routing and account last fours, wire or ACH reference, amounts, dates, and the last four of the account that funded it. Do not delete the thread yet. Detail: evidence to keep.

Call your bank’s fraud line

Use the number on the back of your card or the official bank site — not a number from the chat. Say romance scam or romance fraud. Ask them to cancel a pending send, start a wire recall, or request an ACH return. Speed matters more than a long love story.

Report the profile, then FTC ReportFraud and IC3

Block and report on the app or social site. US: ReportFraud.ftc.gov and IC3.gov. UK: Action Fraud. Australia: Scamwatch. Filings compile names, account last-fours, and scripts; they are not a refund desk. Checklist: how to report a dating scam.

Tell someone you trust

Shame is how the second ask lands — a “failed refund,” a new hospital invoice, or a firm that says they can get the wire back. A trusted friend reduces the chance you send again.

Recovery-scam warning

After a bank-transfer or wire loss, a second crew often arrives — by text, email, or a site that ranks for “wire transfer dating scam recovery” or “ACH romance scam refund.” They pose as lawyers, bank “fraud specialists,” government agents, or private tracers. They say the IMAD or ACH trace is located. They need a retainer, a tax, a processing fee, or another wire “to release the refund.”

The FTC’s line is short: do not trust someone who contacts you claiming they can recover lost money for a fee. Government agencies and legitimate organizations will not ask for money or financial details — and will never ask for a bank transfer, Zelle, Western Union, or gift cards — in exchange for a refund. Anyone who does is a scammer. Sometimes it is the same operators in a new costume.

  • They found you. You did not look up the number yourself.
  • They want payment first — retainer, unlock, tax, or another ACH / wire.
  • They want remote access or your bank / email login.
  • They guarantee a percentage back if you act today.
  • They tell you not to talk to the bank because it would “void the seizure.”

There is no private firm that can reverse a posted wire you already authorized, and no official who needs another bank transfer to release a hold. If a recovery pitch already took more money, treat that as a new scam: stop, call the bank again, file another ReportFraud and IC3 report. Do not hire a third person to fix the second. FAQ

What is a bank transfer romance scam?

Someone you met online asks you to send a bank transfer — ACH, a domestic wire, or “send to my account and I’ll refund you” — for an emergency, a visit, customs, an investment, or a fake overpayment. Legitimate partners and agencies do not get paid that way. Flags: dating scam red flags. Hub: romance scams.

What is an ACH romance scam vs a wire-transfer dating scam?

Same crime, different speed. A wire is usually same-day and treated like cash once paid out. ACH uses routing and account numbers and may take overnight or post the same day. Crews pick whichever clears tonight. Cash pickup: Western Union. P2P: Zelle / Cash App / Venmo.

Why do scammers want a bank transfer instead of a card?

A transfer you start is usually labeled authorized. There is no merchant chargeback. The receiving account can empty fast. The story about “flags” or “the hospital only takes a wire” is cover.

Is “send to my account and I’ll refund you” ever real?

Treat it as bait. Real partners do not invoice you by routing number before you have met. A promised Friday refund, a screenshot of an inbound that is not in your bank app, and “pay a release fee to get your money back” are the usual shapes. Check your official app — not a link from the chat.

Can I get a bank wire or ACH back?

Usually hard once it has posted and been paid out. Still call the bank the same day — pending sends are sometimes stopped. Do not pay a recovery firm. Limits: get money back from a scam. Already sent: I sent money to a scammer.

Someone said they can recover my bank-transfer loss for a fee. Should I pay?

No. A retainer, tax, or second wire “to release the refund” is a recovery scam. Legitimate agencies never charge to recover a transfer. Detail: recovery-scam warning.

What should I do the same day?

Stop sending. Screenshot chat, confirmation, and receiving details. Call the bank fraud line. Report and block the profile. File FTC ReportFraud and IC3. Steps: how to report.
