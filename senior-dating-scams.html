Senior dating scams: when a match on a dating or social app asks an older adult for money

A calm handbook for romance fraud aimed at older adults — not a scare page, and not the broad elderly-scams hub. How the dating-app arc works, the scripts that keep showing up, and what to do now.

Date Safely editors Updated Sep 13, 2026 18+

Quick answer

Senior dating scams — also searched as elderly dating scams — are romance frauds that start on a dating or social app, move the chat off-app, then ask for money. OurTime, SilverSingles, Match, Facebook, a “wrong number” text: the door changes; the sequence does not. Never send gift cards, wires, Zelle, or crypto to someone you have not met. If money already left: bank the same day, then FTC ReportFraud and IC3. Do not pay anyone who offers to get it back. Broader elder hub (tech support + family emergency): elderly scams. Romance hub: romance scams explained.

Key takeaways

  • This page is the dating / romance angle. Virus pop-ups and “your grandson is in jail” live on the elderly scams hub.
  • Off-app chat plus no live video plus a money story is enough to stop. You do not have to disprove the deployment or the hospital first.
  • Gift cards, wires, payment apps, and crypto are the steal. Amount is not the test.
  • If cash left: stop, bank same day, report, tell one trusted person. A paid “recovery” is the second steal.

What senior dating scams are — and what this page is not

People search senior dating scams, elderly dating scams, and romance scams targeting seniors after a parent sent money to someone they met on a dating site — or after a chat that already felt wrong. Those phrases are the same crime with different search boxes. Definition: what is a romance scam.

The FTC and FBI describe romance fraud the same way for every age: someone pretends to be a romantic partner so they can get paid. On this beat the door is a dating profile or a social-app message. OurTime, SilverSingles, Match, Plenty of Fish, Facebook, Instagram, or a swipe app. Warmth first. A move to WhatsApp, Google Chat, or iMessage. Then a favor that can only be paid in gift cards, a wire, Zelle, or crypto. Types map: online dating scams. Website-inbox version: dating website scams.

This page stays on that dating / romance door. It does not replace the caregiver hub that also covers a tech-support pop-up and a family-emergency or grandparent call. Those costumes use the same rails. They are not this script. Hub: elderly scams.

Ordinary dating after 60 is still ordinary. Plenty of people meet on these apps and stay in the original inbox until they have done unscripted live video. Distance is not the crime. The money ask is.

Why older adults are targeted

Age is not a character flaw. Operators look for conditions, not a stereotype of “gullible.” Three show up again and again — and none of them are a moral failing.

  • Isolation after a loss. Widowhood, divorce, a move, a smaller circle. A daily chat can feel like company before it feels like a con. The script is written to become the only conversation in the house.
  • Savings that can move in one transfer. A lifetime of retirement accounts, a house that is paid off, a habit of helping. The first ask is small on purpose. The later ask uses the same trust.
  • Politeness norms. A lifetime of answering the phone, finishing the conversation, and not wanting to be rude. “I should at least hear them out” is the delay the rail needs. You can hang up. You can refuse a gift-card PIN. That is not cruelty.

FTC older-consumer reports list romance fraud among the categories where adults 60 and over lose money more often than younger adults — alongside tech support, prizes, and government impersonation. AARP’s public romance-scam series puts dating apps and social media as common starting points and names secrecy as a warning sign, not a private romance. Shame is part of the design. People wait. Waiting is how a $200 gift card becomes a wire you cannot recall.

You do not have to be lonely, online-naive, or “bad with phones” for this to land. Anyone who will take a dating conversation seriously is in the pool. The useful response is practical, not a lecture about who should be dating.

Common scripts

The costume changes. The payment instruction does not. These five lines show up constantly once a senior dating chat has left the app. How the first request is usually worded: romance scam scripts. Composite stories: romance scam examples.

  • Military or deployed contractor. A soldier, sailor, or overseas worker cannot video “for security.” Leave papers, a ticket home, kit, or a held package appear next. Real US service members are not billed through a dating chat. Full pattern: military romance scams.
  • Customs or a package. Gold, documents, or a gift is “coming to you” if you pay a release fee — often in gift cards. There is no package. Paying can also be used to recruit you as a money mule.
  • Crypto or an investment after romance. Weeks of warmth, then a trading screenshot, a “friend’s platform,” or an unlock fee to withdraw a fake profit. That long version is pig butchering, not a hot tip from someone who loves you. Dating-app arc: pig butchering on dating apps. Rail-focused page: crypto romance scams.
  • Gift cards. iTunes, Google Play, Steam, Target, Amazon, Vanilla. “Just this once.” The numbers are cash. Nobody’s hospital, army, or visa office collects Apple codes. Handbook: gift card scams.
  • Emergency travel. A wreck, a sudden hospital bill, a missed flight, a locked hotel, “so I can finally come see you.” The visit keeps slipping. The payment deadline does not. Amount is not the test.

Other lines use the same rails: a verification fee so the dating profile “stays visible,” a tax to release a romance refund, or a “temporary loan” until a contract pays. Nobody in a match thread can charge you to stay on OurTime or Match. If the site itself billed a membership you did not want, that is a cancel problem, not this page: how to cancel dating apps.

Red flags checklist

You do not need every item. Overseas or always traveling + no live video + a money story is enough to walk away. Longer lists: romance scam red flags · dating scam red flags.

  • They will not do unscripted live video — or they only send a clip. “Broken camera,” “no FaceTime on base,” a frozen still. Distance is the product. Stolen and generated photos are common. Photo checks: how to spot fake profiles.
  • They rush off the dating app the same day. WhatsApp, Google Chat, Telegram, or a personal email. The report button stays behind on purpose.
  • They ask you not to tell family. “They won’t understand us.” Isolation is the setup, not a private romance. A real match can survive a second adult knowing the name.
  • Urgency plus an irreversible payment. Gift cards, wires, Zelle / Cash App, crypto, or a courier for cash. “Tonight or I lose the ticket.” A real emergency can survive a call to your bank or your children. This script cannot.
  • The story slips; the money pitch stays smooth. Age, city, branch of service, or the visit date changes. The PIN or wallet address does not.

You do not owe them a debate about the deployment or the hospital.

Real people who want a relationship can handle “I stay on the app until live video” and “I do not send money to people I have not met.” If that boundary ends the chat, the chat needed to end.

What to do now

Order matters more than a perfect write-up. If you are still holding a gift card, put it back. If a transfer is pending, call the bank before you reread the thread.

Stop sending

No last gift card. No customs hold. No unlock fee. No “secure account” transfer. More money does not buy the earlier money back, and it does not make a real partner appear at the airport. Do not send one last message to catch them. They already know.

Call the bank or card same day

Use the number on the back of the card — not a number from the chat. Say romance scam or romance fraud. Flag wires, Zelle, gift-card purchase points, crypto on-ramps, and any cash handoff. Speed matters more than a long love story.

Report the profile, then FTC and IC3

Block and report on the dating or social app. US: ReportFraud.ftc.gov and IC3.gov. UK: Action Fraud. Australia: Scamwatch / ReportCyber. Those filings compile scripts and wallets; they are not a refund desk. Checklist: how to report a dating scam.

Save evidence and tell one person you trust

Screenshots of the profile, chat, wallet addresses, URLs, and receipts — before you block if you can. Shame is why people wait and why caregivers find out late. One trusted person reduces the chance a second script lands.

What a dispute can still do, and what gift cards and crypto usually cannot: how to get money back from a scam.

If you are family or a caregiver

Adult children and caregivers often file the report that shows the real loss. The useful moves are practical, not a lecture about who should be online. Dating after a loss is not the mistake. The irreversible payment is.

  • Believe the payment rail, not the story. Gift cards, a secrecy ask, and no live video beat a debate about whether “he really is on a base in Syria.”
  • Call the bank with them, same day. Sit in the room. The fraud desk can sometimes freeze a transfer that is still sitting. Use the number on the card, not a number from the chat.
  • Do not take over the phone in a way that isolates them again. The next script will say you “won’t understand” or that you want the inheritance. Stay on the same side of the table. Give them a job: screenshots, the receipt, the case number.
  • A sentence that is enough from them: “I sent money to someone I met online. I have stopped. I need help reporting it and I do not want to send more.” They do not have to replay every message tonight.
  • Watch for the recovery call that follows. Lists of people who already paid — and of the relatives who called the bank — get sold. The section below is for you too.

If you need a calm script for the first money ask they are still reading: romance scam scripts. If images or a leak threat are in the mix, that is sextortion — do not pay. Help: how to report a dating scam.

Tech-support pop-ups and “Grandma, I need bail” are a different door with the same rails. Use elderly scams for those, then come back here if a dating profile is also in the mix.

Recovery-scam warning

After a senior dating loss, a second crew often arrives. They pose as lawyers, crypto tracers, “victim advocates,” or even the FTC or FBI. They say the money is located. They need a retainer, a tax, a processing fee, or remote access “to deposit the refund.” Caregivers get the same pitch: “we can get your mother’s money back if you pay today.”

The FTC’s line is short: do not trust someone who contacts you claiming they can recover lost money for a fee. Government agencies and legitimate organizations will not ask for money or financial details in exchange for a refund. Anyone who does is a scammer. Sometimes it is the same operators in a new costume.

  • They found you — you did not look up the agency yourself.
  • They want payment first — retainer, unlock, tax, “clearance,” another gift card.
  • They want remote access or a bank / wallet / email login.
  • They tell you not to talk to family or the bank because it would “tip off” the first scammer.

Paying them does not recover the first loss. It funds the second. If a recovery pitch already took more money, treat that as a new scam: stop, call the bank again, file another ReportFraud and IC3 report. Do not hire a third person to fix the second. Full map: get money back from a scam.

FAQ

What are senior dating scams?

Romance frauds aimed at older adults. Someone builds a relationship on a dating or social app, moves off-app, then asks for gift cards, wires, payment apps, or crypto. Elderly dating scams and senior romance scams are the same pattern. Hub: romance scams explained.

How is this different from the elderly scams page?

This handbook stays on dating-app and romance-dating fraud. The elderly scams hub also covers tech-support pop-ups and family-emergency or grandparent calls. Same rails; different doors.

Why would someone target an older adult on a dating app?

Isolation after a loss, savings that can move in one transfer, and a habit of answering politely. Age is not the flaw. FTC older-consumer reports list romance fraud among the categories where adults 60+ lose money more often than younger adults. Flags: romance scam checklist.

What should I do if I already sent money?

Stop. Call the bank or card the same day. Report and block the profile. File FTC ReportFraud and IC3. Tell one trusted person. Do not pay a recovery firm. Steps: get money back from a scam · how to report.

Should I pay a company that says they can get the money back?

No. A retainer, tax, or unlock fee is the second steal. Legitimate agencies never charge for a refund. Anyone who does is a scammer — including people who call the caregiver. Detail: recovery-scam warning.
