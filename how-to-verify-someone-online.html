How to verify someone you met on a dating app — before money, or a meet

A calm identity check, not a scare page. Reverse-search the photos, start an unscripted live call, keep the chat on the app, and treat any gift-card or crypto “verify” ask as the steal.

Date Safely editors Updated Sep 13, 2026 18+

Quick answer

To verify someone you met online: run their photos through Google Images, TinEye, and Yandex; stay on the dating app; start a live video call yourself and ask for motion they could not have filmed; then look for a social history that is older than the match. A blank reverse search is unknown, not a pass. Never send gift cards, crypto, wires, or an ID selfie to “verify.” If the story slips, stop. Walk-away list: dating scam red flags. Photo tells: how to spot fake profiles.

Key takeaways

  • Stolen photos still light up reverse search. Generated faces often don’t. Blank = unknown.
  • You start the call. Unscripted motion. A gym clip is inventory.
  • Stay on the app until that check. WhatsApp drops the report button.
  • Nobody bills you gift cards or crypto so they can prove they are real.
  • When the city, job, or face fails the check, you do not owe them another chance.

People search how to verify someone online after a match who will not turn on a camera — or after a face that looked too even. This is the checklist for that moment. It is not a background check, and it is not a promise the person is kind. It is how you find out whether the photos, the voice, and the story belong to the same human before you leave the inbox that can still report them.

Ordinary dating still uses filters and good lighting. Distance is not the crime. The irreversible payment is. App-door patterns: dating app scams. Generated faces and chatbot warmth: AI dating scams.

Reverse image search — Google, TinEye, and Yandex

Reverse image search is still the first tool. It is no longer the last. Stolen photos of real people still show up under another name, on a model’s Instagram, or on a news site. Generated faces often leave nothing to match because the image never lived on the public web. A blank result used to feel like a pass. Treat it as unknown.

Run the same photo on all three. They do not index the same web.

Google Images

  1. Go to images.google.com — type the URL yourself.
  2. Camera icon → upload a screenshot or paste the image URL if the app gives you one.
  3. Look for the same face with another name, a stock-photo site, or a different city.

TinEye

TinEye is older and narrower. That is useful. It is good at finding the first time a photo was posted, and at cropped or compressed copies. If Google is noisy, TinEye is often cleaner. Same rule: a hit under another identity is enough. No matches prove nothing.

Yandex

Yandex Images often catches faces Google misses — especially photos that circulated in Eastern Europe or on sites Google ranks poorly. Romance crews reuse the same stolen military and “oil rig” portraits across regions. If you only search Google, you can miss the original.

  • Same face, different name — done. That is a catfish or a stolen-photo script. Do not debate it in the chat.
  • Same face, same name, old posts — useful, not proof. Anyone can copy a public life.
  • No matches — unknown. Common for AI faces. Inconclusive. Go to live video.
  • They tell you to “Google me, I’m not famous” — they are borrowing the old advice. A blank search is not a character reference.

Crop to the face if the background is a stock beach. Search more than one photo. A single “safe” shot next to a stolen or generated extra is a common mix. Photo-first tells: how to spot a fake dating profile.

Stay on the app until verified

The first urgency in a scam chat is often leaving the inbox. “The app keeps logging me out.” “WhatsApp is easier.” Dating apps log reports and can ban a crew’s numbers. WhatsApp and Telegram do not owe you that. Stay on Tinder, Bumble, Hinge, or the site you matched on until you have done live video you started.

If they need to move off-app before a five-minute call, that is data. You do not owe them a preference debate. Walk-away list: red flags. App-specific hinge: dating app scams.

You do not owe them a debate about the app.

Real people who want a relationship can handle “I stay here until we do a live call” and “I do not send money to people I have not met.” If that boundary ends the chat, the chat needed to end.

Video-call pressure tests — you start the call

A gym clip, a “good morning” video, or a voice note is not verification. Operators recycle footage. Face-swap tools can hold a short, fixed angle. Your job is to force spontaneity on a tool you opened — in-app video when the dating app offers it.

You place the call

Do not join a link they pasted from a new domain. Prefer the dating app’s own video if it exists. A call they schedule and you only watch is still their clip.

Ask for unscripted motion

In the moment: wave the left hand across the face, turn 90°, hold up three fingers, say a nonsense word, show the book on the table. Pre-recorded video cannot adapt. Current face-swaps still glitch on occlusion and a sudden turn.

Keep it clothed and short

You are checking identity, not performing intimacy. Recording risk is real. Intimate video, then a threat, is a different clock — do not pay. Playbook: webcam blackmail.

Two short calls beat one polished one-off

Ask again on a different day. Scripted clips are cheap. Repeat, unscripted presence is not. A camera that “breaks” the week money talk starts is the same data as a refused wave.

Tinder or Hinge photo verification means a face matched a selfie check at some point. It does not mean the person in the chat is that face. Useful. Not a background check. Deepfake and chatbot costume: AI dating scams.

Never send money, crypto, or gift cards to “verify”

This is the line that matters more than a perfect photo search. Nobody who is who they say they are needs iTunes, Target, Steam, a wire, Zelle, Cash App, or a wallet address so you can “know they’re real.” Hospitals, shippers, and exchanges do not collect gift-card PINs from a dating match.

  • “Verify on this page” — a link to confirm your card, unlock the match, or join an “invite-only” app. Dating apps do not send you to a random domain to verify a crush. Type official URLs yourself. Stay in-app.
  • “Send a card so I know you care” — the first PIN is a test of whether the rail works. Amount is not the test. A $50 “just this once” is the same crime as a larger ask.
  • Crypto, a trading screenshot, or an unlock fee — that is pig butchering, not a hot tip. A small withdrawal that “works” is the hook.
  • Your driver’s license or a selfie with a handwritten note — identity theft, not romance. Do not mail a copy of your ID to prove you are real.

Live video reduces catfish risk. It does not make a stranger safe to pay. If cash already left: bank the same day, then FTC ReportFraud and IC3. Map: how to get money back from a scam.

Social-profile cross-checks

A real person usually leaves a trail that is older than the dating-app account: tagged photos, coworkers, a local café, a year of messy stories. Burners are tidy. Look for the life outside the grid — not a display name that matches the chat.

  • Old Instagram or Facebook with tagged photos — better than a two-week grid of six studio shots. Tags from other people are harder to fake than a bio.
  • LinkedIn with a company that exists and coworkers who post — a brand-new profile with no connections is a costume. Do not treat a job title as proof.
  • The name, city, and employer line up across places — or they don’t. Details that slip while the affection stays smooth are a catfish tell. Money version: catfishing scam.
  • They refuse any public footprint — privacy is real. So is a crew that only exists in your thread. Live video still has to happen. Secrecy that blocks a five-minute call is the product.

Do not send them your own full name, workplace, or family tree to “prove trust” before they have done the same live check. Isolation — “don’t tell your friends, they won’t understand us” — protects the rail, not the relationship.

When the story fails verification

You do not need a courtroom. One strong fail is enough: stolen photos, a refused live gag, a gift-card “verify,” a city that changes. Shame is why people keep chatting. Waiting is how a $50 card becomes a wire.

Stop sending

No last gift card. No ID photo. No unlock fee. No “one last” WhatsApp to catch them. They already know. More money does not buy the earlier money back.

Save evidence, then report and block

Screenshot the profile, photos, chat, wallet addresses, and URLs before you block if you can. Report in the app so other people stop matching them. Taps: how to report a dating scam.

If money already left

Call the bank or card the same day. Say romance scam or romance fraud. US: ReportFraud.ftc.gov and IC3.gov. UK: Action Fraud. Australia: Scamwatch / ReportCyber. Recovery limits: get money back from a scam.

Tell one person you trust

A second adult knowing the name reduces the chance a new “verification” script lands. You were targeted. That is not the same as being foolish.

Anyone who contacts you later offering to recover the money for a fee is running a second steal. Government agencies do not ask for gift cards or crypto to process a refund.

FAQ

How do I verify someone I met on a dating app?

Reverse-search the photos on Google Images, TinEye, and Yandex. Stay on the app. Start a live call yourself and ask for unscripted motion. Cross-check social accounts for a history older than the match. Never send gift cards, crypto, or wires to “verify.”

Does a reverse image search prove a dating profile is real?

No. A hit under another name is enough to walk away. A blank search is inconclusive — stolen photos still match; generated faces often don’t. Pressure-test with live video you start. Photo checks: spot fake profiles.

What is a video-call pressure test?

You start the call. Ask for a wave across the face, a turn, a word you invent. A clip they send, a frozen angle, or a camera that “breaks” when you ask for motion is not that check. AI / deepfake costume: AI dating scams.

Should I send money or gift cards to verify a dating match?

No. Gift cards, crypto, wires, Zelle, Cash App, and unlock fees are the steal. Dating apps do not collect card numbers in a match thread. Investment-romance version: pig butchering on dating apps.

What should I do if their story fails verification?

Stop. Save screenshots. Report and block in the app. If cash left, call the bank the same day and file FTC ReportFraud and IC3. Checklist: how to report a dating scam · get money back.
