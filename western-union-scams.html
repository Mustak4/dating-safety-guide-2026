Western Union scams: when a dating match asks for a wire or cash pickup

A calm handbook for romance and dating chats that ask you to send Western Union, MoneyGram, Ria, Walmart2Walmart, or a bank wire — to get home, clear customs, cover a hospital, unlock leave papers, fund an “investment,” or reverse a fake refund.

Date Safely editors Updated Sep 13, 2026 18+

Quick answer

A Western Union or MoneyGram scam in dating is a fake relationship (or a sudden crisis) built to get you to send cash that can be picked up in minutes. The brand on the receipt changes — Western Union, MoneyGram, Ria, Walmart2Walmart, a bank wire. The rail does not. Once someone collects it, it behaves like cash. Stop sending. Screenshot the chat, the MTCN or wire reference, and the receiver name. Call the sending agent and your bank the same day, then file FTC ReportFraud and IC3. Anyone who offers to get the Western Union money back for a fee is running the second scam. Hour-zero if cash already left: I sent money to a scammer. Recovery limits: get money back from a scam.

Key takeaways

  • Real partners, hospitals, customs offices, the military, and brokers do not collect Western Union, MoneyGram, Ria, or a personal wire from a dating chat.
  • Cash pickup is the point: an MTCN or reference number, often sent to a “cousin” or “agent” who is not the face in the photos.
  • Same-day cancel attempt + bank fraud line beats a perfect write-up next week. Keep screenshots.
  • Anyone who offers to recover the Western Union money for a fee is running the second scam.

What a Western Union / MoneyGram romance ask is

A Western Union scam in a dating or romance context is simple: someone you have not met in person — often after warmth on Tinder, Bumble, Hinge, Instagram, or a “wrong number” text — asks you to send money they can collect as cash or as a bank wire. Western Union and MoneyGram are the names people type. Ria, Walmart2Walmart, and a walk-in bank wire do the same job. This page is one handbook for that rail, not five thin stubs.

The story may be “I need to get home,” a customs hold, a hospital deposit, military leave papers, an investment account that “only takes a wire,” or a package that will unlock after you pay. The FTC’s public line is blunt: do not wire money or send a cash transfer to someone you have not met in person. Government agencies, banks, hospitals, and legitimate companies will not demand you pay a dating match that way. Neither will a real romantic partner who respects you. Broader map: dating scam red flags. P2P version of the same steal: Zelle scams · PayPal scams. Gift-card version: gift card scams. Customs costume: customs-fee scams.

The dollar amount is not the test. A $90 “handling” charge and a $4,000 “last ticket home” are the same crime. You do not owe them a debate about why Western Union is “the only thing that posts tonight.”

How cash-pickup and wire romance asks work

These rails are built to move value to a person who can walk into an agent, a Walmart, or a bank and leave with cash — or to a receiving account that can empty the same day. Romance crews use that design.

  • Irreversible cash pickup. Western Union, MoneyGram, Ria, and Walmart2Walmart are designed so a receiver can collect in minutes. Once the cash is paid out, the sending company usually cannot pull it back. That is the product, not a glitch.
  • The MTCN (or reference number) is the key. Western Union calls it a Money Transfer Control Number. MoneyGram and Ria use a similar reference. They will ask you to text the number, a photo of the receipt, the exact receiver name, and the pickup city. That packet is how a stranger collects your money.
  • “Send it to my cousin / agent / colleague.” The name on the transfer is often not the name on the dating profile. They say the cousin is closer to an agent, the CO will not let them leave base, or “my broker has to receive it.” A different person picking up cash is the tell, not a favor.
  • A bank wire is the same idea in a suit. A domestic or international wire to “the hospital account,” “the investment desk,” or “customs” is still a person-to-person send. Once the receiving bank pays it out, recall windows are short. There is no merchant chargeback button.

The logo on the receipt is not the partner.

Western Union, MoneyGram, Ria, Walmart2Walmart, a credit-union wire — crews rotate brands when one agent starts asking questions. “Only this one works tonight” is pressure, not logistics. Isolation (“don’t tell your bank or your sister”) protects the rail.

Quoted scripts

You do not need a word-for-word match. Grammar got better. The cash-pickup rail did not. These are the shapes victims keep quoting — airport and customs, hospital, military leave, an investment wire, and a fake refund that needs money sent back out.

Airport / customs hold

“I’m at the airport. Customs is holding my kit — they only take Western Union for the duty. $480 to this name, this city. I’ll be on the next flight. Don’t tell your family; they won’t understand the travel rules.”

Why it works: the visit is the prize; the fee is the price of seeing them. The tell: customs offices and airlines do not collect Western Union from a stranger on a dating chat. A tracking number you cannot verify on a site you typed yourself is inventory. Gift-card and “package” costumes of that ask: gift card scams · customs-fee scams.

Hospital / medical

“Baby I’m at the ER and they need a deposit before they treat me. MoneyGram is the only thing that posts tonight. $700 to this name — it’s my cousin; he’s closer to the agent. I’ll pay you back Friday when payroll hits. Please don’t tell anyone — I’m so embarrassed.”

Why it works: medical urgency plus shame plus a brand you already trust from the grocery store. The tell: hospitals do not collect MoneyGram from a dating match. Real emergencies go through insurance, a person who can see you, or a card — not a WhatsApp handle and a cousin’s ID. A lockout, a lawyer, or a broken-down rental is the same script with a new costume.

Military leave papers

“My leave is approved but they won’t stamp the papers until the processing fee is paid. Western Union to my CO’s agent — I’ll send the name. I just want to come home to you. Don’t post anything; ops security.”

Why it works: duty, secrecy, and a visit that is always next week. The tell: the U.S. military does not invoice a civilian on a dating app for leave, travel, or equipment. A real service member can video on a tool you start. Soldier costume of the same invoice: military romance scams.

Investment “broker” needs a wire

“My broker opened the account in both our names. The desk only takes a bank wire — not Zelle, not a card. $2,400 today and you’ll see it in the portal tonight. I’ll walk you through the login after you send the reference number.”

Why it works: it sounds like a grown-up financial step, not a gift. The tell: a real brokerage does not collect a personal wire from a dating-app match, and it does not need you to bypass card rails. A portal you did not type yourself is inventory. Crypto and fake-trading version of that steal: pig butchering on dating apps.

Fake refund / overpayment, then a reverse wire

“I sent $3,200 by mistake — I meant $320 for the ticket. Wire $2,880 back Western Union so I can pick it up today. Keep the rest. The bank already cleared it on my side.”

Why it works: you feel you are holding their money. The tell: the inbound payment is often stolen, a fake screenshot, or a check that will bounce. If you send a cash pickup “back,” you eat the loss when the first payment disappears. Do not refund anyone you met online. Open your bank app yourself — not a link from the chat. PayPal costume of that bait: PayPal scams.

You do not owe them a debate about the agent location.

Real people who want a relationship can handle “I do not send Western Union, MoneyGram, or wires to people I have not met” and “I stay on the app until live video.” If that boundary ends the chat, the chat needed to end.

Why these rails are hard to reverse

Credit-card merchant disputes exist because a card network sits between you and a business. Cash-pickup transfers and most bank wires do not work that way. Romance crews pick the rail with no chargeback button.

  • It is treated like cash. You usually signed a send form or clicked Send. Fraud desks often label that “authorized.” Say romance scam or romance fraud — not “I paid a friend” or “I helped my partner get home.”
  • Pickup can happen before you leave the store. A completed Western Union, MoneyGram, Ria, or Walmart2Walmart payout is typically gone. A pending transfer is sometimes canceled if you and the sending agent move the same hour.
  • A bank wire has a short recall window. Once the receiving bank has paid it out, the sending bank usually cannot claw it back. Same-day is the whole strategy. There is no Visa/Mastercard chargeback path on a person-to-person wire.
  • The receiver name is a mule, not a merchant. “Cousin,” “agent,” “broker,” and a slightly misspelled legal name are how the cash leaves the agent window. You cannot dispute a store the way you dispute a charged hotel.
  • Shame keeps people quiet long enough for the trail to go cold. Waiting is how a $200 “duty” becomes a second wire you cannot recall.

Still call the transfer company and the linked bank or credit union the same day. Ask them to cancel or recall it. Zelle, PayPal Friends & Family, gift-card codes, and crypto have the same finality once they leave. Compare rails: how to get money back from a scam. What to do the same day

Order matters more than a perfect write-up. If a transfer is still pending, call the sending agent or bank before you reread the thread.

Stop sending

No “one last” Western Union. No second brand because the first “didn’t clear.” No customs hold. No leave-paper fee. No correction wire. More money does not buy the earlier money back. They already know.

Screenshot everything

Chat (dating app and off-app), profile URLs or handles, the MTCN or MoneyGram / Ria reference, receiver name and city, agent location, confirmation screens, amounts, dates, and the last four of the card or account that funded it. Do not delete the thread yet.

Call the transfer company and your bank

Use a number from the official Western Union, MoneyGram, Ria, Walmart, or bank site — or the number on the back of your card — not a number from the chat. Say romance scam or romance fraud. Ask them to cancel a pending pickup or start a wire recall. Speed matters more than perfect wording.

Report the profile, then FTC ReportFraud and IC3

Block and report on the app or social site. US: ReportFraud.ftc.gov and IC3.gov. UK: Action Fraud. Australia: Scamwatch. Filings compile names, MTCNs, and scripts; they are not a refund desk. Checklist: how to report a dating scam.

Tell someone you trust

Shame is how the second ask lands — a “failed pickup,” a new airport tax, or a firm that says they can get the Western Union money back. A trusted friend reduces the chance you send again.

Recovery-scam warning

After a Western Union, MoneyGram, or wire loss, a second crew often arrives — by text, email, or a site that ranks for “Western Union scam recovery.” They pose as lawyers, transfer-company “fraud specialists,” government agents, or private tracers. They say the MTCN is located. They need a retainer, a tax, a processing fee, or another Western Union / MoneyGram send “to release the refund.”

The FTC’s line is short: do not trust someone who contacts you claiming they can recover lost money for a fee. Government agencies and legitimate organizations will not ask for money or financial details — and will never ask for Western Union, MoneyGram, a wire, Zelle, or gift cards — in exchange for a refund. Anyone who does is a scammer. Sometimes it is the same operators in a new costume.

  • They found you. You did not look up the number yourself.
  • They want payment first — retainer, unlock, tax, or another cash-pickup send.
  • They want remote access or your bank / email / transfer-account login.
  • They guarantee a percentage back if you act today.
  • They tell you not to talk to the bank or Western Union because it would “void the seizure.”

There is no private firm that can reverse a cash pickup you already funded, and no official who needs more Western Union to release a hold. If a recovery pitch already took more money, treat that as a new scam: stop, call the bank again, file another ReportFraud and IC3 report. Do not hire a third person to fix the second. FAQ

What is a Western Union or MoneyGram scam in dating or romance?

Someone you met online asks you to send Western Union, MoneyGram, Ria, Walmart2Walmart, or a bank wire — for an airport hold, customs, a hospital, military leave, an investment, or a refund. Legitimate partners and agencies do not get paid that way. Flags: dating scam red flags.

Why do scammers want Western Union, MoneyGram, or a bank wire?

Cash pickup and wires move fast and are hard to reverse. There is no card chargeback. The story changes. The rail stays the same. Parallel irreversible rails: Zelle · PayPal · gift cards.

What is an MTCN, and why do they say “send it to my cousin”?

An MTCN is Western Union’s pickup code. MoneyGram and Ria use a reference number that does the same job. “Send it to my cousin / agent” means a different person collects the cash. That is the steal, not a favor.

Can I get a Western Union, MoneyGram, or wire transfer back?

Usually hard once it is picked up or paid out. Still call the sending agent and your bank the same day — pending sends are sometimes canceled. Do not pay a recovery firm. Limits: get money back from a scam. Already sent: I sent money to a scammer.

Someone said they can get my Western Union money back for a fee. Should I pay?

No. A retainer, tax, or second wire “to release the refund” is a recovery scam. Legitimate agencies never charge to recover a transfer. Detail: recovery-scam warning.

What should I do the same day?

Stop sending. Screenshot chat, MTCN or wire reference, and receipts. Call the transfer company and the bank. Report and block the profile. File FTC ReportFraud and IC3. Steps: how to report.
