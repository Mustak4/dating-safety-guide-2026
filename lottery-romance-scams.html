Lottery and inheritance romance scams: the unexpected prize, then the fee

A calm handbook for dating chats that claim a lottery win, inheritance, insurance payout, or unexpected prize — then ask you to pay taxes, lawyer fees, a release fee, or customs so the money can be claimed. There is no payout. The fee is the steal.

Date Safely editors Updated Sep 13, 2026 18+

Quick answer

A lottery or inheritance romance scam is a fake relationship built to get you to pay a tax, lawyer fee, release fee, or customs hold so a prize, estate, or insurance check can be claimed. Searches for lottery dating scams and inheritance romance scams are the same pattern. There is no win. The FTC’s line on prizes is blunt: if you have to pay to get it, it is a scam. Never send it. Ask for live video. If cash already left: bank the same day, then FTC ReportFraud and IC3. Hour-zero: I sent money to a scammer. Recovery limits: get money back from a scam.

Key takeaways

  • Real lotteries, estates, and insurers never collect taxes or release fees through a dating chat, gift cards, Western Union, or crypto.
  • Lottery, inheritance, insurance, and “I already paid — just the last fee” are costumes. The rail is the crime.
  • Love bomb + secret windfall + a fee tonight is enough to walk away. You do not have to disprove the ticket or the will.
  • If cash left: stop, bank same day, report, FTC, and IC3. Anyone who charges to recover funds is the second scam.

What a lottery / inheritance romance ask is

This is a romance scam with a winning ticket. Someone you have not met — after warmth on Tinder, Bumble, Hinge, Instagram, or a “wrong number” text — says they hit a lottery, inherited from an overseas relative, have an insurance payout sitting, or won a prize they never entered. Then a tax office, a lawyer, a notary, or a customs desk needs a fee before the money can move. You send the money. The prize never turns up.

People also type lottery dating scam, inheritance scam dating, and inheritance romance scam. Those are aliases for this page — not separate cons. Lottery vs estate vs insurance vs “Publishers Clearing House” is costume. Advance-fee means you pay first so something (a check, a transfer, a visit) can be released. There is nothing to release. Amount is not the test. A $90 “processing” charge and a $4,200 “inheritance tax” are the same crime.

The job story can change. A deployed soldier, an oil-rig contractor, or “I’m stuck overseas” all invoice the same hold — sometimes the prize is theirs, sometimes they say it is already in your name. Sister costumes: military romance scams · oil-rig romance scams. The package version of the same invoice: customs-fee scams. The rail is often gift cards or a wire — why those rails are cash: gift card scams · Western Union scams.

Paying can also be used to recruit you as a money mule — they ask you to receive a “prize check” and send the leftover on. That is a second crime, not a favor. Check costume of that bait: fake-check scams. Walk-away list: dating scam red flags.

How lottery / inheritance romance asks work

The ask is late on purpose. Early suspicion would end the chat. The usual order is love bomb → secret windfall → fee to release → a new rail when the first one is refused.

  • Love bomb. Fast intimacy on the dating app, then WhatsApp, Telegram, or Google Chat so the report button stays behind. They remember the dog’s name. They talk about a visit after “this is settled.” Some crews play it slower now. The warmth is inventory.
  • Secret windfall. A lottery they “didn’t expect,” an uncle in another country, a life-insurance policy, a prize they never entered. Isolation is built in: “Don’t tell your family — they will think I am using you,” or “the lawyer said we have to keep this quiet until the tax is paid.”
  • Fee to release. Taxes, lawyer or notary fees, a release fee, customs on a “certificate,” an insurance processing charge. The visit or the shared future is always next month. The payment deadline is tonight. The FTC’s public line on prizes is short: if you have to pay to get it, it is a scam.
  • Escalate the rails. Gift cards first (iTunes, Google Play, Steam, Target). Then Western Union or MoneyGram because “the tax office only takes cash pickup.” Then a payment app or crypto “because it’s faster.” Each refusal becomes a new emergency. Irreversible on purpose.

You do not need the full four steps. Secret money + no live video + a fee is enough. You do not have to disprove the ticket number or the will. Broader map: dating scam red flags.

The logo on the “lottery letter” is not the partner.

A PDF that looks like a state lottery, a law-firm letterhead, a notary stamp, an insurance claim ID — crews rotate brands when one story starts getting searched. “Only send the tax tonight” is pressure, not logistics. Isolation protects the rail, not the relationship.

Quoted scripts

These are composites of lines victims quote to banks and to the FTC. You do not need a word-for-word match. Grammar got better. The invoice did not. The useful part is irreversible payment, tonight, so a prize you cannot look up will be “released.”

Lottery tax

“I won $2.4 million in the interstate lottery — I bought the ticket on a stopover. They will not release the check until someone pays $1,180 in winner’s tax. Google Play / iTunes is how the claims office takes it. I already paid the first installment. Please — once it clears I am flying to you and we share everything. Don’t tell your sister; I don’t want this public.”

Why it works: a shared future, plus shame, plus a number that looks official. The tell: real prizes are free. Lotteries withhold tax through official channels, or you pay it on a return — they do not collect iTunes codes from a civilian on WhatsApp. A ticket number you cannot verify on a site you typed yourself is inventory. Gift-card rail: gift card scams.

Lawyer / notary release

“My solicitor in London needs a notary fee and a release bond before they can wire the estate into a joint account for us. I am offshore and cannot sign. Western Union to the name on the invoice — I will send the letterhead. Once you pay, they release it this week.”

Why it works: it sounds like grown-up paperwork, not a gift. You are helping them come home. The tell: real probate lawyers do not invoice a dating-app match by Western Union, and they do not need Target cards to “stamp” a will. A PDF letterhead is not a law firm. Wire costume: Western Union scams. Soldier or contractor version of the stranded-travel line: military romance scams · oil-rig romance scams.

Inheritance from an overseas relative

“My uncle in [country] died. I am the only heir but I am not a citizen there. The estate is $800,000 in a bonded account. They need you — as my fiancée — to pay the inheritance tax and a small customs fee on the documents so they can transfer it to your bank. I will add your name. We will be set for life.”

Why it works: you are written into the story as family. The money is “already yours.” The tell: the FTC’s inheritance / long-lost-relative alerts are the same product: there is no policy and no estate. Foreign probate does not bill a civilian’s Zelle or a wallet. Paying can also recruit you as a mule — receiving a transfer and sending it on is a second crime. Package costume of the same hold: customs-fee scams.

Insurance payout

“The life-insurance claim is approved. The company will not cut the check until a beneficiary on this side pays the processing fee and a tax. I put your name because I trust you. Deposit is $640 — Apple Cash or a money order they can track. Then we split it when I land.”

Why it works: an approved claim sounds finished. You are doing a favor, not buying a dream. The tell: insurers do not collect processing fees through a dating chat, and they do not need a match you met last month as a beneficiary overnight. A “claim number” in WhatsApp is inventory. Fake-check version of the leftover send: fake-check scams.

“I already paid — just cover the last fee”

“I already paid the lawyer and the first tax from my side. They moved the prize. There is one last release fee — $280 — and then it is done. I am so close. Please, just this. If you wait they send it back and I lose everything I already put in.”

Why it works: sunk cost. You feel you would waste their money by pausing. The tell: there were no earlier payments. “Last fee” is a product line. A second and third “last” fee is how the steal grows. More money does not buy the earlier money back, and it does not make a real check appear. If cash already left: I sent money to a scammer.

You do not owe them a debate about the ticket or the will.

Real people who want a relationship can handle “I do not send money to people I have not met” and “I need unscripted live video.” If that boundary ends the chat, the chat needed to end.

Why real lotteries and estates never ask strangers on dating apps for fees

Credit-card merchant disputes exist because a card network sits between you and a business. A “winner’s tax” sent as gift-card codes does not work that way. Romance crews pick rails that are hard to reverse and stories that make the send feel temporary.

  • Real prizes are free. The FTC’s prize and lottery pages are blunt: if someone tells you to pay taxes, shipping, processing, or customs to get a prize, they are a scammer. State lotteries and real sweepstakes do not collect through WhatsApp. They do not take iTunes, Steam, or a Western Union pickup name you were texted.
  • Estates and insurers do not bill a match. Probate, withholding, and claim payouts run through courts, licensed counsel, and the company that wrote the policy. A PDF “solicitor” who needs you to wire a release bond tonight is not that. The FTC’s long-lost-relative inheritance and life-insurance alerts describe the same lie without the dating-app wrapper.
  • Gift cards, Western Union, and crypto are cash. Codes, cash pickup, and a wallet you do not control are the steal. No legitimate lottery, tax office, or estate uses those rails because they cannot be reversed once they leave. Why those rails are chosen: gift card scams · Western Union / wires.
  • A dating chat is not a claims desk. Real agencies do not need a fiancée they have never met to “sponsor” a tax. They do not ask you to keep it from your bank or your sister. Secrecy and urgency — “the window closes tonight” — are the product.
  • You are not required to disprove the windfall. Overseas + no live video + a fee is enough. A serial, a claim ID, or a “cleared” screenshot they send is inventory. Live, unscripted video that you start is the identity check — not a deposit.

Still call the bank the same day if money already left, and still try to stop a pending send. Wires, Zelle, PayPal Friends & Family, gift-card codes, and crypto have the same finality once they post. Compare rails: how to get money back from a scam.

What to do the same day — stop, bank, FTC, IC3

Order matters more than a perfect write-up. If you are still holding a gift card, put it back. If a transfer is pending, call the bank before you reread the thread.

Stop sending

No last tax. No second “release.” No smaller notary fee as a compromise. No “one last” WhatsApp to catch them. More money does not buy the earlier money back, and it does not make a real prize or estate appear.

Call the bank or card same day

Use the number on the back of the card — not a number from the chat. Say romance scam or romance fraud. Flag wires, Zelle, gift-card purchase points, crypto on-ramps, and any cash handoff. Speed matters more than a long love story. What a dispute can still do: get money back from a scam.

Report the profile, then FTC ReportFraud and IC3

Block and report on the app or social site so other people stop matching them. US: ReportFraud.ftc.gov and IC3.gov. UK: Action Fraud. Australia: Scamwatch / ReportCyber. Those filings compile scripts and wallets; they are not a refund desk. Checklist: how to report a dating scam.

Save evidence, tell someone you trust

Screenshots of the profile, chat, fake lottery or law-firm PDFs, wallet addresses, URLs, and receipts — before you block if you can. Shame is why people wait. One trusted person reduces the chance a second “the prize moved to another claims office” script lands.

If the send already posted: I sent money to a scammer.

Recovery-scam warning

After a lottery, inheritance, or prize-fee loss, a second crew often arrives — by text, email, or a site that ranks for “lottery romance scam recovery.” They pose as lawyers, claims officers, customs brokers, government agents, or “victim advocates.” They say the prize is located, the wire is frozen, or the tax can be refunded. They need a retainer, a tax, a processing fee, or your bank login “to deposit the winnings.”

The FTC’s line is short: do not trust someone who contacts you claiming they can recover lost money for a fee. Government agencies and legitimate organizations will not ask for money, gift cards, or crypto in exchange for a refund. Anyone who does is a scammer. Sometimes it is the same operators in a new costume.

  • They found you. You did not look up the number yourself.
  • They want payment first — retainer, unlock, tax, “AML clearance,” more gift cards or USDT.
  • They want remote access or your bank / exchange / wallet login.
  • They guarantee a percentage back if you act today.
  • They tell you not to talk to family or the bank because it would “tip off” the first scammer or “void the claim.”

There is no private firm that can reverse a PIN or a transfer you already signed, and no official who needs iTunes cards to release a lottery hold. If a recovery pitch already took more money, treat that as a new scam: stop, call the bank again, file another ReportFraud and IC3 report. Do not hire a third person to fix the second. Recovery hub: get money back from a scam. Same-day sequence if you already paid: I sent money to a scammer.

FAQ

What is a lottery or inheritance romance scam?

A fake relationship that uses a lottery win, inheritance, insurance payout, or unexpected prize as the reason you must pay taxes, lawyer fees, a release fee, or customs. There is no prize. Lottery dating scams and inheritance romance scams are the same pattern. Flags: dating scam red flags.

Would a real lottery or estate ask me to pay taxes via gift cards or Western Union?

No. Real prizes are free. Legitimate lotteries, probate offices, and insurers do not collect through a dating chat, gift cards, wires, payment apps, or crypto. Gift-card and wire rails: gift card scams · Western Union scams.

They say they already paid and I just need to cover the last fee. Is that different?

No. “I already paid — you only cover the last tax / notary / customs hold” is the same invoice. The earlier fees were never paid. A second “last” fee is how the steal grows. Detail: quoted scripts.

What should I do if I already paid a tax, lawyer, or release fee?

Stop. Do not pay a second release or recovery fee. Call the bank or card the same day, report the profile, then file FTC ReportFraud and IC3. Steps: report a dating scam · I sent money to a scammer.

Should I hire a lawyer or recovery firm who found the prize?

No. A retainer, tax, or unlock fee is the second steal. Legitimate agencies never charge for a refund. Anyone who does is a scammer. Detail: recovery-scam warning.

How is this different from a customs-fee or fake-check ask?

Same crime, different costume. Package or airport hold: customs-fee scams. A check you are told to deposit: fake-check scams. This page is the windfall — lottery, inheritance, insurance, or prize — then a fee so it can be claimed.
