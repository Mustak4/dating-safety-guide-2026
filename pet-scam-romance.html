Pet and puppy romance scams: the stranded dog, then the fee

A calm handbook for dating chats that claim a pet or puppy is stranded, needs shipping, a vet bill, customs, or a kennel release. There is no animal. The fee is the steal.

Date Safely editors Updated Sep 13, 2026 18+

Quick answer

A pet or puppy romance scam is a fake relationship built to get you to pay shipping, a vet bill, customs, or a kennel hold so an animal can be “released” or sent to you. Searches for puppy scam dating, pet shipping scam, and romance pet scam are the same pattern. There is no puppy. Real shelters and airlines never collect through a dating chat, gift cards, Western Union, or crypto. Never send it. Ask for live video. If cash already left: bank the same day, then FTC ReportFraud and IC3. Hour-zero: I sent money to a scammer. How they hide: how to spot a romance scammer.

Key takeaways

  • Real shelters, vets, airlines, and customs desks never collect through a dating chat, gift cards, Western Union, or crypto.
  • Stranded puppy, vet bill, kennel release, customs on the crate, and “I already paid half” are costumes. The rail is the crime.
  • Love bomb + pet-crisis photos + a fee tonight is enough to walk away. You do not have to disprove the kennel.
  • If cash left: stop, bank same day, report, FTC, and IC3. Anyone who charges to recover funds — or the dog — is the second scam.

What a pet / puppy romance ask is

This is a romance scam with a crate. Someone you have not met — after warmth on Tinder, Bumble, Hinge, Instagram, or a “wrong number” text — says a puppy, a dog they “rescued,” or “my last companion” is already on the way, or stuck at a cargo desk, a vet, or a boarding kennel. Then shipping, a vet bill, customs, or a kennel hold needs a fee before the animal can move. You send the money. The pet never turns up.

People also type puppy scam dating, pet shipping scam, and romance pet scam. Those are aliases for this page — not separate cons. Puppy vs cat vs “my service dog at the airport” is costume. Advance-fee means you pay first so something (a crate, a visit, a release) can move. There is nothing to release. Amount is not the test. A $70 “handling” charge and a $2,800 “quarantine” are the same crime.

The job story can change. A deployed soldier, an oil-rig contractor, or “I’m stuck overseas” all invoice the same hold — sometimes the pet is “theirs,” sometimes they say it is already in your name as a gift. Sister costumes: military romance scams · oil-rig romance scams. The package version of the same invoice, without the animal: customs-fee scams. The windfall version: lottery / inheritance romance scams. The rail is often gift cards or a wire — why those rails are cash: gift card scams · Western Union scams.

Paying can also be used to recruit you as a money mule — they ask you to receive a “kennel refund” or a leftover check and send it on. That is a second crime, not a favor. Check costume of that bait: fake-check scams. Walk-away list: how to spot a romance scammer.

How pet / puppy romance asks work

The ask is late on purpose. Early suspicion would end the chat. The usual order is love bomb → pet-crisis photos → shipping / vet / customs fee → a new rail when the first one is refused.

  • Love bomb. Fast intimacy on the dating app, then WhatsApp, Telegram, or Google Chat so the report button stays behind. They remember the dog’s name — or they invent one and ask you to help “bring her home.” They talk about a visit after “this is settled.” Some crews play it slower now. The warmth is inventory.
  • Pet-crisis photos. A crate at an airport, a kennel receipt, a vet clipboard, a puppy in a carrier they “cannot pick up” because they were called back to base or the rig. Photos and clips are stolen or generated. Isolation is built in: “Don’t tell your family — they will think I am using you,” or “the kennel said we have to keep this quiet until the hold is paid.”
  • Fee to release. Shipping, a special crate, a vet or quarantine bill, customs on the live-animal shipment, a kennel or bonded-warehouse release. The visit or the shared future is always next month. The payment deadline is tonight. The FTC’s public line on pet ads is short: only scammers insist you pay with gift cards, a payment app, crypto, or a wire.
  • Escalate the rails. Gift cards first (iTunes, Google Play, Steam, Target). Then Western Union or MoneyGram because “the kennel only takes cash pickup.” Then a payment app or crypto “because it’s faster.” Each refusal becomes a new emergency — a second vet, a new customs desk, a threat they will “put her down.” Irreversible on purpose.

You do not need the full four steps. Crisis photos + no live video + a fee is enough. You do not have to disprove the tracking number or the kennel. Broader map: how to spot a romance scammer.

The logo on the “kennel invoice” is not the partner.

A PDF that looks like Delta Cargo, a shelter letterhead, a vet stamp, a customs hold ID — crews rotate brands when one story starts getting searched. “Only send the release tonight” is pressure, not logistics. Isolation protects the rail, not the animal — and not the relationship.

Quoted scripts

These are composites of lines victims quote to banks and to the FTC. You do not need a word-for-word match. Grammar got better. The invoice did not. The useful part is irreversible payment, tonight, so a hold you cannot look up will “release” a pet that is not there.

Stranded puppy

“My puppy is stuck at the cargo terminal. I got called back to the rig / the base before I could pick her up. They need $420 tonight or they put her down. Google Play is how the kennel takes payment. I already paid the flight. Please — she is all I have left. Don’t tell your sister; I don’t want this public.”

Why it works: a living thing in danger, plus shame, plus a number that looks official. The tell: real cargo desks and shelters do not collect iTunes or Google Play codes from a civilian on WhatsApp. A crate photo you cannot verify on a site you typed yourself is inventory. Soldier or contractor version of the stranded-travel line: military romance scams · oil-rig romance scams. Gift-card rail: gift card scams.

Vet bill

“The vet found a problem — she needs treatment before they will release her. I am offshore and cannot pay from here. Western Union to the name on the invoice — I will send the letterhead. Once you cover it I am flying home with her and we start our life.”

Why it works: it sounds like grown-up care, not a gift. You are helping an animal, and helping them come home. The tell: real clinics do not invoice a dating-app match by Western Union, and they do not need Target cards to “clear” a chart. A PDF letterhead is not a hospital. Wire costume: Western Union scams.

Kennel release

“The boarding kennel will not release her until the hold is cleared. I already paid half. You only need the release fee so they will put her on the truck to your door. I will add your name as the receiver. We will be a family.”

Why it works: you are written into the story as the person waiting at home. The dog is “already yours.” The tell: boarding kennels bill the owner of record through their own desk — not a fiancée they have never met. Paying can also recruit you as a mule — receiving a “refund” or leftover check and sending it on is a second crime. Package costume of the same hold: customs-fee scams. Check costume: fake-check scams.

Customs on the crate

“Customs has the crate. They need duty on the live-animal shipment or they send her back. iTunes / Target is how the bonded warehouse takes it. Tracking is in the PDF I sent. Please — I cannot lose her, and I cannot get to a bank from here.”

Why it works: an official-sounding hold, plus a tracking number. The tell: real customs and airlines do not collect through a dating chat. The FTC’s line on packages is the same product: if someone tells you to send money to receive a shipment, it is a scam. A “cleared” screenshot they send is inventory. Same invoice without the animal: customs-fee scams.

“I already paid half — need you for the rest”

“I already paid the flight and the first vet bill from my side. They moved her. There is one last kennel fee — $260 — and then she is yours until I land. I am so close. Please, just this. If you wait they put her down and I lose everything I already put in.”

Why it works: sunk cost. You feel you would waste their money — and kill an animal — by pausing. The tell: there were no earlier payments. “Last fee” is a product line. A second and third “last” fee is how the steal grows. More money does not buy the earlier money back, and it does not make a real dog appear. If cash already left: I sent money to a scammer.

You do not owe them a debate about the kennel or the crate.

Real people who want a relationship can handle “I do not send money to people I have not met” and “I need unscripted live video.” If that boundary ends the chat, the chat needed to end.

Why real shelters and airlines never ask strangers on dating apps for fees

Credit-card merchant disputes exist because a card network sits between you and a business. A “kennel release” sent as gift-card codes does not work that way. Romance crews pick rails that are hard to reverse and stories that make the send feel temporary — and kind.

  • Real shelters bill at the shelter. Licensed rescues and municipal shelters collect adoption fees on-site or through a site you typed yourself. They do not invoice a match on WhatsApp. They do not take iTunes, Steam, or a Western Union pickup name you were texted. The FTC’s pet-ad alerts are blunt: only scammers insist on gift cards, a payment app, crypto, or a wire.
  • Airlines and customs do not bill a dating chat. Pet cargo, live-animal holds, and duty run through the shipper of record and official desks. A PDF “bonded warehouse” that needs you to wire a release tonight is not that. The package version of the same lie: customs-fee scams.
  • Gift cards, Western Union, and crypto are cash. Codes, cash pickup, and a wallet you do not control are the steal. No legitimate kennel, vet, airline, or customs desk uses those rails because they cannot be reversed once they leave. Why those rails are chosen: gift card scams · Western Union / wires.
  • A dating chat is not a cargo desk. Real agencies do not need a fiancée they have never met to “sponsor” a crate. They do not ask you to keep it from your bank or your sister. Secrecy and urgency — “they put her down at midnight” — are the product.
  • You are not required to disprove the kennel. Overseas + no live video + a fee is enough. A tracking number, a “cleared” screenshot, or a puppy clip they send is inventory. Live, unscripted video that you start is the identity check — not a deposit. Photo habits: how to spot a romance scammer.

Still call the bank the same day if money already left, and still try to stop a pending send. Wires, Zelle, PayPal Friends & Family, gift-card codes, and crypto have the same finality once they post. Hour-zero if the send already posted: I sent money to a scammer.

What to do the same day — stop, bank, FTC, IC3

Order matters more than a perfect write-up. If you are still holding a gift card, put it back. If a transfer is pending, call the bank before you reread the thread.

Stop sending

No last kennel hold. No second “vet.” No smaller customs fee as a compromise. No “one last” WhatsApp to catch them. More money does not buy the earlier money back, and it does not make a real animal appear.

Call the bank or card same day

Use the number on the back of the card — not a number from the chat. Say romance scam or romance fraud. Flag wires, Zelle, gift-card purchase points, crypto on-ramps, and any cash handoff. Speed matters more than a long love story — or a crate photo. Hour-zero steps: I sent money to a scammer.

Report the profile, then FTC ReportFraud and IC3

Block and report on the app or social site so other people stop matching them. US: ReportFraud.ftc.gov and IC3.gov. UK: Action Fraud. Australia: Scamwatch / ReportCyber. Those filings compile scripts and wallets; they are not a refund desk. Checklist: how to report a dating scam.

Save evidence, tell someone you trust

Screenshots of the profile, chat, fake kennel or cargo PDFs, wallet addresses, URLs, and receipts — before you block if you can. Shame is why people wait. One trusted person reduces the chance a second “the crate moved to another port” script lands.

If the send already posted: I sent money to a scammer.

Recovery-scam warning

After a pet-shipping, vet, or kennel-fee loss, a second crew often arrives — by text, email, or a site that ranks for “puppy romance scam recovery.” They pose as lawyers, customs brokers, airline investigators, shelter officers, or “victim advocates.” They say the crate is located, the wire is frozen, or the duty can be refunded. They need a retainer, a tax, a processing fee, or your bank login “to deposit the refund” or “to release the dog.”

The FTC’s line is short: do not trust someone who contacts you claiming they can recover lost money for a fee. Government agencies and legitimate organizations will not ask for money, gift cards, or crypto in exchange for a refund. Anyone who does is a scammer. Sometimes it is the same operators in a new costume.

  • They found you. You did not look up the number yourself.
  • They want payment first — retainer, unlock, tax, “AML clearance,” more gift cards or USDT.
  • They want remote access or your bank / exchange / wallet login.
  • They guarantee a percentage back — or the puppy — if you act today.
  • They tell you not to talk to family or the bank because it would “tip off” the first scammer or “void the seizure.”

There is no private firm that can reverse a PIN or a transfer you already signed, and no official who needs iTunes cards to release a crate. If a recovery pitch already took more money, treat that as a new scam: stop, call the bank again, file another ReportFraud and IC3 report. Do not hire a third person to fix the second. Same-day sequence if you already paid: I sent money to a scammer.

FAQ

What is a pet or puppy romance scam?

A fake relationship that uses a stranded pet, shipping hold, vet bill, customs on a crate, or kennel release as the reason you must pay a fee. There is no animal. Puppy scam dating, pet shipping scam, and romance pet scam are the same pattern. Flags: how to spot a romance scammer.

Would a real shelter or airline bill a stranger on a dating app via gift cards or Western Union?

No. Real shelters, vets, airlines, and customs desks do not collect through a dating chat, gift cards, wires, payment apps, or crypto. Gift-card and wire rails: gift card scams · Western Union scams.

They say they already paid half and I just need to cover the rest. Is that different?

No. “I already paid — you only cover the rest / last kennel / last customs hold” is the same invoice. The earlier fees were never paid. A second “last” fee is how the steal grows. Detail: quoted scripts.

What should I do if I already paid a shipping, vet, or kennel fee?

Stop. Do not pay a second release or recovery fee. Call the bank or card the same day, report the profile, then file FTC ReportFraud and IC3. Steps: report a dating scam · I sent money to a scammer.

Should I hire a broker or recovery firm who found the puppy?

No. A retainer, tax, or unlock fee is the second steal. Legitimate agencies never charge for a refund. Anyone who does is a scammer. Detail: recovery-scam warning.

How is this different from a customs-fee, military, or oil-rig ask?

Same crime, different costume. Package or airport hold without the animal: customs-fee scams. Soldier story: military romance scams. Contractor story: oil-rig romance scams. This page is the pet pretext — then a fee so the crate can be “released.”
