Fake-check scams: when a dating match sends a check and wants the extra

A calm handbook for romance and dating chats that send (or claim to send) a check, cashier’s check, or money order that looks real, then pressure you to wire, gift-card, Apple Cash, or Western Union the “extra” — or the shipping, customs, or fees — before the check bounces.

Date Safely editors Updated Sep 13, 2026 18+

Quick answer

A fake-check romance scam is a fake relationship (or a sudden pet, package, wedding, or “job”) built to get you to deposit a check that looks real and send the surplus out before your bank reverses it. Mobile deposit can show available funds in hours. That is not the same as the money being real. The FTC’s line is blunt: even if you see the funds, the bank can take them back weeks later — and you owe whatever you already wired. Stop. Do not send the extra. Call the bank fraud desk the same day, then file FTC ReportFraud and IC3. Anyone who offers to recover the bounced check for a fee is running the second scam. Hour-zero if cash already left: I sent money to a scammer. Recovery limits: get money back from a scam.

Key takeaways

  • Real partners, companies, customs desks, and the military do not mail you a cashier’s check from a dating chat and ask you to wire the leftover.
  • “Available” on a mobile deposit is a hold timeline, not a cleared check. The bounce can land days or weeks later. You are on the hook.
  • Same-day bank fraud desk + do not send the surplus beats a perfect write-up next week. Keep screenshots.
  • Anyone who offers to recover the bounced-check loss for a fee is running the second scam.

What a fake-check romance ask is

A fake-check scam in a dating or romance context is simple: someone you have not met in person — often after warmth on Tinder, Bumble, Hinge, Instagram, or a “wrong number” text — sends, or claims they have sent, a check. It may be a personal check, a cashier’s check, a money order, or a photo of one they want you to mobile-deposit. Then they need the leftover sent out tonight. Searches for fake check scam, cashier’s check scam, and money order scam are aliases for this page, not three different cons.

The FTC’s public line is blunt: a person you do not know asks you to deposit a check — usually for more than they owe you — and send some of the money back. They always have a story for why you cannot keep it all. Government agencies, banks, customs offices, and legitimate companies will not run that errand through a dating chat. Neither will a real romantic partner who respects you. Broader map: dating scam red flags. Where the surplus usually leaves: Western Union / wires · gift card scams · Zelle / Cash App / Venmo. Package costume: customs-fee scams.

The dollar amount is not the test. A $180 “shipping” leftover and a $4,800 “company overpay” are the same crime. You do not owe them a debate about why the cashier’s check is “already verified.”

How fake-check romance asks work

These instruments are built to look like money your bank will honor. Romance crews use that design — and the fact that mobile deposit can post before anyone has proven the paper is real.

  • Mobile deposit posts first. Collection comes later. You photograph the check. The app says “available” the same day or the next business day. Federal availability rules push banks to release funds quickly, especially on cashier’s checks. That credit is provisional. The paying bank can still return the item.
  • The bounce is delayed on purpose. Counterfeits and stolen accounts can take days or weeks to reject. The FTC says it can take weeks to discover and untangle a fake. By then the surplus you wired or gifted is gone, and your bank takes the full deposit back.
  • Overpayment is the classic hinge. A ticket, a pet, a “loan repayment,” or a visit costs $400. The check is $2,400. “Deposit it and send me the rest — I’ll explain to payroll.” You feel you are holding their money. You are holding a future debit.
  • Pet, package, wedding, military leave — same invoice, new costume. A puppy “already shipped,” a wedding-dress box at customs, a deposit for a venue they “can’t pay from overseas,” leave papers that need a processing fee. The check covers the hold. You send shipping, duty, or the leftover. There is no puppy, no dress, no leave stamp. Soldier costume of that invoice: military romance scams. Duty costume: customs-fee scams.
  • The surplus leaves on a rail with no chargeback. Western Union, MoneyGram, gift-card codes, Apple Cash, Zelle, PayPal Friends & Family. That is the steal. The check is only the bait that makes the send feel like “their” money.

The logo on the check is not the partner.

A cashier’s-check watermark, a money-order serial, a credit-union routing number — crews rotate brands when one bank starts asking questions. “Only send the extra tonight” is pressure, not logistics. Isolation (“don’t tell your bank or your sister”) protects the bounce window.

Quoted scripts

You do not need a word-for-word match. Grammar got better. The bounce timeline did not. These are the shapes victims keep quoting — deposit-then-send-the-rest, customs on the package, a company “overpay,” and a cashier’s check or money order they swear is already verified.

Deposit it, then send me the rest

“I sent a cashier’s check for $3,200 — I meant $320 for the ticket. Deposit it and send me the rest Western Union / Apple Cash so I can pick it up today. Keep a little. The bank already cleared it on my side. Please don’t tell anyone — I’m so embarrassed I wrote the extra zero.”

Why it works: you feel you are holding their money, plus shame, plus a visit that is always next week. The tell: nobody who wrote a real check needs you to wire the leftover to a cousin tonight. The inbound item is often stolen or counterfeit. When it reverses, the surplus you sent is your loss. Do not refund anyone you met online. Wire costume of that bait: Western Union scams. PayPal costume: PayPal scams.

Customs / shipping on the package

“The puppy / the dress / my kit is sitting at customs. I already mailed you a money order for the duty plus a little extra. Deposit it, pay the $380 release, and send me what’s left. They only take gift cards or Western Union at that warehouse. I’ll be on the next flight.”

Why it works: the visit or the pet is the prize; the fee is the price of seeing it. The tell: customs offices and couriers do not collect Google Play or Western Union from a dating chat, and they do not fund the duty with a money order they mailed you first. A tracking number you cannot verify on a site you typed yourself is inventory. Gift-card and “package” costumes: gift card scams · customs-fee scams.

“My company overpaid you”

“HR / my contractor sent you a check by mistake — they overpaid the per diem / the equipment stipend in your name. Deposit it. Keep $200 for the trouble. Wire the rest back to the office account I text you. Payroll already verified the check. If you wait they freeze both of us.”

Why it works: it sounds like a grown-up payroll error, not a gift. You are doing them a favor. The tell: a real company does not payroll a civilian they met on a dating app, and it does not need you to strip the extra out by gift card or Zelle. A “verified” check they narrate in WhatsApp is not a bank confirmation. You may also be used as a money mule — receiving and forwarding is a second crime, not a favor.

Money order / cashier’s check “already verified”

“It’s a cashier’s check — those can’t bounce. I had my bank verify the serial. Or it’s a USPS money order; look up the number. Mobile-deposit it, wait until you see available, then send the leftover on Apple Cash. That’s how we know you’re real before I fly.”

Why it works: people still believe cashier’s checks and money orders are cash. Mobile deposit “available” looks like proof. The tell: counterfeit cashier’s checks and money orders are printed to look real, including bank names. A serial you “looked up” on a link they sent is inventory. Apple does not confirm a dating match by leftover Cash. Live, unscripted video that you start is the identity check — not a deposit. Apple Cash costume of the leftover: Apple Pay scams.

You do not owe them a debate about the check clearing.

Real people who want a relationship can handle “I do not deposit checks from people I have not met” and “I do not send the leftover.” If that boundary ends the chat, the chat needed to end.

Why “available funds” is not real money

Credit-card merchant disputes exist because a card network sits between you and a business. A deposited check does not work that way. Romance crews pick the gap between available and collected.

  • Availability is a clock, not a verdict. The FTC is blunt: by law, banks have to make deposited funds available quickly. Seeing the money in the app does not mean it is a good check. A cashier’s check often posts the next business day. A mobile deposit can show a balance the same afternoon. Collection — the paying bank actually honoring the item — can take much longer.
  • The bounce can land days or weeks later. Counterfeits, stolen accounts, and altered money orders are not always caught at the teller window or in the photo. When the item comes back, your bank debits the full amount. If you already sent the surplus, you now owe the bank and you are out the wire, the codes, or the Apple Cash.
  • You are responsible for the paper you deposited. Fraud desks often hear “but it cleared.” Say romance scam or fake-check fraud — not “I paid my partner back” or “I refunded an overpay.” The surplus send is usually labeled “authorized” because you clicked Send.
  • A “verified” cashier’s check is the costume. People trust the watermark. Crews print the watermark. A money-order receipt photo is the same trick. The bank that “verified” it in the chat is not your bank, and a number from the match is not the fraud desk.
  • Shame buys the extra week. Waiting is how a $200 “shipping” leftover becomes a second gift-card run after the first deposit “didn’t post.” Isolation (“don’t tell your sister”) is how the bounce window stays open.

Still call the bank the same day if the check is in, and still try to stop a pending surplus send. Wires, Zelle, PayPal Friends & Family, gift-card codes, and Apple Cash have the same finality once they leave. Compare rails: how to get money back from a scam. What to do the same day

Order matters more than a perfect write-up. If you have not deposited the check, do not. If a surplus send is still pending, call that company before you reread the thread.

Do not send the surplus

No “one last” wire. No gift cards because the first “didn’t cover duty.” No Apple Cash “correction.” No second check. More money does not make the first instrument real, and it does not make a real partner appear. They already know.

If it is not deposited, leave it alone

Do not mobile-deposit it. Do not take it to a teller “just to see.” Do not cash a money order at a grocery store. A photo in the chat is enough evidence. You do not need to test the paper.

Call the bank fraud desk if it is already in

Use the number on the back of your card or the official bank site — not a number from the chat. Say romance scam or fake-check fraud. Ask them to flag the deposit, hold remaining funds, and note that any leftover send was induced. Speed matters more than a long love story. If you already wired or gifted the extra, call that company the same hour and ask to cancel a pending pickup or freeze unused codes.

Screenshot everything

Chat (dating app and off-app), profile URLs or handles, front and back of the check or money order, mobile-deposit confirmation, amounts, dates, the surplus rail (MTCN, $Cashtag, Apple Cash, gift-card PINs), and the last four of the account. Do not delete the thread yet.

Report the profile, then FTC ReportFraud and IC3

Block and report on the app or social site. US: ReportFraud.ftc.gov and IC3.gov. UK: Action Fraud. Australia: Scamwatch. Filings compile scripts and check images; they are not a refund desk. Checklist: how to report a dating scam.

Tell someone you trust

Shame is how the second ask lands — a “failed deposit,” a new customs hold, or a firm that says they can get the bounced check back. A trusted friend reduces the chance you send again.

Recovery-scam warning

After a fake-check, cashier’s-check, or money-order loss, a second crew often arrives — by text, email, or a site that ranks for “fake check scam recovery.” They pose as lawyers, bank “fraud specialists,” government agents, or private tracers. They say the check is located or the bounce can be reversed. They need a retainer, a tax, a processing fee, or another wire / gift-card send “to release the refund.”

The FTC’s line is short: do not trust someone who contacts you claiming they can recover lost money for a fee. Government agencies and legitimate organizations will not ask for money or financial details — and will never ask for Western Union, gift cards, Zelle, or Apple Cash — in exchange for a refund. Anyone who does is a scammer. Sometimes it is the same operators in a new costume.

  • They found you. You did not look up the number yourself.
  • They want payment first — retainer, unlock, tax, or another surplus send.
  • They want remote access or your bank / email / mobile-deposit login.
  • They guarantee a percentage back if you act today.
  • They tell you not to talk to the bank because it would “void the seizure” or “tip off payroll.”

There is no private firm that can make a counterfeit collect, and no official who needs more gift cards to release a hold. If a recovery pitch already took more money, treat that as a new scam: stop, call the bank again, file another ReportFraud and IC3 report. Do not hire a third person to fix the second. FAQ

What is a fake-check scam in dating or romance?

Someone you met online sends — or claims to send — a personal check, cashier’s check, or money order, then tells you to deposit it and send the extra, the shipping, or the fees back out. Legitimate partners and agencies do not work that way. Flags: dating scam red flags.

Why does my bank show the money if the check is fake?

Banks must make funds available quickly. “Available” is not the same as collected. A counterfeit can take days or weeks to bounce. You still owe the bank — and whatever surplus you already sent. Detail: why available funds are not real money.

Is a cashier’s check or money order “verified” safe?

No. Those instruments are counterfeited because people trust them. A teller glance or a mobile-deposit photo is not authentication. A “verified” text from the match is the script. Do not send the leftover.

Can I get money back after I already sent the surplus?

The deposit reverse is a bank conversation. The surplus wire, gift cards, or Apple Cash are usually hard once they post. Still call the bank and that rail the same day. Do not pay a recovery firm. Limits: get money back from a scam. Already sent: I sent money to a scammer.

Someone said they can recover my bounced-check loss for a fee. Should I pay?

No. A retainer, tax, or second wire “to release the refund” is a recovery scam. Legitimate agencies never charge to recover a fake-check loss. Detail: recovery-scam warning.

What should I do the same day?

Do not send the surplus. Do not deposit if you have not. If it is already in, call the bank fraud desk. Screenshot the chat and the check. Report and block the profile. File FTC ReportFraud and IC3. Steps: how to report.
