Gift card scams: when a dating match asks for iTunes, Steam, or Target cards

A calm handbook for romance and dating gift-card asks — not a scare page. What the request looks like, why the codes are cash, the scripts that keep showing up, and what to do now.

Date Safely editors Updated Sep 13, 2026 18+

Quick answer

A gift card romance or dating scam is a fake relationship built to get you to buy cards and send the numbers. iTunes, Google Play, Steam, Target, Amazon, Vanilla — the brand is costume. The codes are cash. Stop buying. Keep the cards and receipts. Call the issuer and your bank the same day, then file FTC ReportFraud and IC3. Hub: romance scams explained. If cash already left: how to get money back from a scam.

Key takeaways

  • Nobody you met on a dating app needs iTunes or Steam cards for rent, medicine, leave, or a crypto wallet.
  • Once you share the numbers or a photo of the back, treat the balance as cash that already moved.
  • “Emergency,” “customs fee,” “unlock my wallet,” and “military leave” are scripts — not separate crimes.
  • Primary moves: stop buying, keep receipts, bank / issuer, FTC ReportFraud, IC3. Never pay a recovery firm.

What a gift-card romance or dating ask looks like

People search gift card scams, romance scam gift cards, and dating scam gift cards after the same sequence: a match on Tinder, Bumble, Hinge, or social, days or weeks of warmth, a move to WhatsApp or Telegram, then a request that sounds small and temporary. Buy a card. Photograph the back. Read the PIN. “Just this once, I’ll pay you back when I land.”

The FTC’s line is short: gift cards are for gifts, not payments. No hospital, army, bank, or government agency collects iTunes or Google Play numbers. A dating match who tells you how to pay — and the how is a gift card — is running the steal, not asking a favor. Broader pattern: romance scams. App-surface version: dating app scams.

The first card is often $25–$100. That is a test of whether the rail works. If you send it, the story grows: a second card, a larger denomination, a different brand “because the first one failed.” Amount is not the test. A $50 Steam card from someone you have not met is the crime.

  • The chat got private fast. “The app keeps logging me out.” The report button stays behind on purpose.
  • They cannot do unscripted live video, or they only send clips. Identity stays optional while money talk starts.
  • They name the brand and the store. Apple, Google Play, Steam, Target, Walmart, Amazon, Vanilla. Sometimes they stay on the phone while you stand at the rack.
  • They want the numbers, not the plastic. A photo of the back, a typed PIN, or a voice note reading the code. That is the cash-out.
  • They ask you not to tell anyone. “Your family won’t understand us.” Isolation protects the rail.

Why gift cards are irreversible

A credit-card charge can sometimes be disputed. A gift-card code cannot. The moment you share the numbers, the operator can drain the balance through a reseller, a game account, or a cash-out site. There is no “recall” button. That is why the script insists on this rail instead of a dinner you could cancel.

Issuers treat a shared PIN like cash you handed over. Some companies will still try to freeze an unused balance if you call the same day with the receipt and the card number. That window is hours, not weeks. Waiting because the chat “felt real” is how a first card becomes a stack.

Still report. The store receipt, the last four of your debit card, and a photo of the unused plastic are what the issuer and the FTC can use. Do not buy a replacement card because someone offered to “trace” the first. That is the second steal — see recovery-scam warning.

Crypto and wires are the other irreversible rails. If the gift-card story later becomes a trading dashboard or an unlock fee, that is the long version: pig butchering scams.

You do not owe them a debate about the store.

Real people who want a relationship can handle “I do not buy gift cards for people I have not met.” If that sentence ends the chat, the chat needed to end. You do not have to disprove the hospital, the customs hold, or the deployment first.

Common scripts

The costume changes. The payment instruction does not. These four lines show up constantly in romance and dating gift-card asks. First-ask language on the broader hub: romance scams.

  • “Emergency.” Hospital bill, car impound, rent tonight, a sick parent, a child’s school fee. They need Apple or Target cards because “the hospital won’t take a wire from overseas” or “my bank is frozen.” Emergencies that can only be paid in iTunes are not emergencies.
  • “Customs fee.” A package, gold, documents, or a gift is “held” until you pay a release charge — in gift cards. There is no package. Paying can also be used to recruit you as a money mule. Same costume on the soldier script: military romance scams.
  • “Unlock my crypto wallet.” A small “test” card, then a larger one, so they can withdraw a frozen profit or verify a Coinbase-looking site. The dashboard is fake. More cards do not unlock coins. That long con: pig butchering.
  • “Military leave.” A deployed soldier, sailor, or contractor needs leave papers, a ticket, kit, or a phone charger. Real US service members are not billed through a dating chat. Uniform is cover; the cards are the steal. Full pattern: military romance scams.

Other lines use the same rail: a visa so they can visit, a verification fee so the dating profile “stays visible,” or a tax to release a romance refund. Nobody in a match thread can charge you to stay on Tinder or Bumble. App-specific version: dating app scams.

What to do now

Order matters more than a perfect write-up. If you are still in the store, put the cards back. If the codes already left, stop buying the next ones.

Stop buying and stop sending

No “one last” card. No second brand because the first “failed.” No photo of a new PIN to “release” the old one. More codes do not buy the earlier codes back. Do not send one last message to catch them. They already know.

Keep the cards and the receipts

Photograph the front and back, the store receipt, and the last four of the card you used to pay. If a balance might still be unused, take the plastic back to the store the same day and ask them to freeze it. Call the issuer with the number on the card. Speed is the whole strategy.

Call the bank or card same day

Say romance scam or gift card scam. Flag the purchase point (Target, Walmart, a grocery till, Apple Cash). If a debit card funded the rack, ask for a fraud review. Wording helps the desk more than a long love story.

Report the profile, then FTC and IC3

Block and report on the app or social site. US: ReportFraud.ftc.gov and IC3.gov. UK: Action Fraud. Australia: Scamwatch / ReportCyber. Those filings compile PINs, numbers, and scripts; they are not a refund desk. Checklist: how to report a dating scam.

How to talk to family

Shame is part of the design. The script told you not to tell anyone because a second adult would have named the gift-card rail. You do not need a courtroom opening. You need one person who can sit with you while you call the store and the bank.

A sentence that is enough: “I sent gift-card codes to someone I met online. I have stopped. I need help reporting it and I do not want to send more.” You do not have to prove you were in love, justify how long it lasted, or replay every message. Facts first: brands, amounts, dates, whether a receipt still exists.

  • Ask for a practical job — a ride to the store, a second set of ears on the issuer call, help saving screenshots. Lectures can wait until the cards are frozen.
  • If they are angry, that is common. Anger is not a reason to disappear back into the chat. Isolation is how a second PIN gets sent at 1 a.m.
  • You can set a boundary on the story. “I will answer questions tomorrow. Tonight I need the receipt and the fraud number.”
  • If you are the family member: believe the payment rail, not the love story. Help them stop and file. Do not pay a “recovery” stranger on their behalf.

If the pressure included intimate images or a leak threat, that is a different clock. Do not pay. Save the threat and use the reporting steps on how to report a dating scam.

Recovery-scam warning

After a gift-card loss, a second crew often arrives. They pose as lawyers, gift-card tracers, consumer advocates, or even the FTC. They say the PINs can be frozen if you pay a retainer, a tax, or buy one more card “so they can process the refund.”

The FTC’s line is short: do not trust someone who contacts you claiming they can recover lost money for a fee. Government agencies and legitimate organizations will not ask for money, gift cards, or crypto in exchange for help getting a refund. Anyone who does is a scammer. Sometimes it is the same operators in a new costume.

  • They found you. You did not look up the number yourself.
  • They want payment first — retainer, unlock, tax, “anti-money-laundering clearance,” another card.
  • They want remote access or your bank / email / wallet login.
  • They guarantee a percentage back if you act today.
  • They tell you not to talk to your bank or family because it would “tip off the original scammer.”

There is no private firm that can reverse a PIN you already shared, and no official who needs Steam cards to release a seizure. If a recovery pitch already took more money, treat that as a new scam: stop, call the bank again, file another ReportFraud and IC3 report. Do not hire a third person to fix the second. FAQ

What is a gift card romance or dating scam?

A fake relationship used to get you to buy gift cards and send the numbers or a photo of the back. The dating-app or WhatsApp warmth is the setup. The codes are the steal. Hub: romance scams explained. App door: dating app scams.

Can I get the money back?

Treat shared codes as cash. Keep the cards and receipts. Call the issuer and the store the same day. File FTC ReportFraud and IC3. Some issuers can freeze an unused balance. Do not pay a tracer. Limits: get money back from a scam.

Why gift cards instead of a wire?

The numbers move like cash and are hard to reverse. A first small card tests whether you will pay. Later scripts — emergency, customs, military leave, unlock a wallet — use the same rail in a louder voice. Soldier costume: military romance scams. Dashboard version: pig butchering.

Should I pay a recovery firm?

No. A retainer, tax, or “one more card to process the refund” is the second steal. Real help is the issuer, the store, your bank, FTC ReportFraud, and IC3 — not a stranger who found you after the first loss. Steps: report a dating scam.
