Crypto romance scams: the money ask after dating-app warmth

A calm handbook for the Bitcoin-and-USDT version of romance fraud — not a scare page. How it overlaps with pig butchering, how the ask usually arrives, and what to do now.

Date Safely editors Updated Sep 13, 2026 18+

Quick answer

A crypto romance scam is a fake relationship built to get you to send cryptocurrency. Pig butchering is the long, platform-based version of the same steal. The warmth on a dating app or WhatsApp is not the product. The wallet is. Stop depositing. Do not pay an unlock fee. Call the bank or exchange the same day, then file FTC ReportFraud and IC3. Dating-app funnel: pig butchering on dating apps. Hub: romance scams explained.

Key takeaways

  • The chat is bait. A wallet address, fake exchange, or “test trade” is the steal.
  • Pig butchering overlaps: same warmth, then a dashboard. A shorter crypto ask can skip the dashboard entirely.
  • Fake exchanges, unlock fees, and guaranteed charts are enough to walk away. You do not need every flag.
  • Primary moves: stop depositing, call the bank, report. Anyone who charges to recover funds is the second scam.

What a crypto romance scam is

A crypto romance scam is a staged romance — often starting on Tinder, Bumble, Hinge, Instagram, or a “wrong number” text — whose purpose is a cryptocurrency transfer. The person you think you are dating asks you to buy Bitcoin, USDT, or another coin and send it to a wallet they control, or to deposit into a site they sent you.

The FTC and FBI describe the same core as other romance scams: a relationship you have not tested in person, a move off the original app, then money on rails that are hard to reverse. Crypto is the rail they prefer because a completed transfer is usually final.

The amount is not the test. A $200 “gas fee” and a $40,000 “margin top-up” are the same crime. You do not owe them a debate about the market.

Crypto romance scam vs pig butchering

Searchers treat these as two queries. Operators often run them as one crew. The useful split is the shape of the steal, not how sincere the chat felt.

  • Overlap. Both start with warmth. Both leave the dating app. Both end in coins you will not get back. Both follow with unlock fees and, later, people who offer “recovery” for a retainer. Law enforcement’s pig-butchering writeups are describing a crypto romance con with extra machinery.
  • Pig butchering is the long industrial version: weeks or months of attention, a fake trading or “wealth management” site, a small withdrawal that actually arrives, then larger deposits, then a frozen balance. The Mandarin name (sha zhu pan) is about fattening before slaughter. Full hub: pig butchering scams explained. Dating-app start: pig butchering on dating apps.
  • A shorter crypto romance ask can skip the dashboard. After a few days of WhatsApp, they need Bitcoin for a visa, a customs hold, a hospital, a joint apartment, or “so we can invest together” — and they text a wallet address. No charts. Still a crypto romance scam.

If you have a login on a site they sent you, treat it as pig butchering and stop. If you only sent coins to an address they pasted, you are still in the same family. The first rule does not change: never send crypto to someone you met online.

How the money ask usually appears

The ask is late on purpose. Early suspicion would end the chat. The usual order:

  1. Dating-app warmth. Polished photos (stolen or generated), a job that explains odd hours or travel, patient replies. They remember the dog’s name.
  2. Off-platform. WhatsApp, Telegram, or iMessage — “the app keeps logging me out,” “this is more private.” The report button stays on Tinder. They want it behind you.
  3. A life that includes trading. Screenshots of a green dashboard. A “mentor.” A friend who “got them in.” Or a sudden emergency that, they say, only crypto can fix tonight.
  4. The first coins. A small deposit, a “test withdrawal” that lands in your real exchange, or a wallet address for a one-off help. That success is engineered. It is not proof the rest is real.
  5. Scale, then friction. Larger deposits. Then an unlock fee, a tax, a KYC hold, or a site that 404s. Then pressure, or silence.

You do not need the full five steps. The moment returns, wallets, or a platform you did not type into the browser yourself appear, the romance is over as a dating question. It is a money question. Walk-away list: dating scam red flags and the longer romance scam checklist.

Red flags — fake exchanges, unlock fees, dashboards

Use this as a stop list, not a quiz. One strong flag is enough.

  • A trading platform you did not find yourself. Invite link, APK, Telegram bot, or a URL that looks like Coinbase, Binance, or Kraken with a letter swapped. Real brokers do not onboard you from a dating chat.
  • An investment dashboard with guaranteed returns. Smooth equity curves, “can’t lose” language, a private fund, or “our group’s platform.” Charts in a screenshot are not a regulated account.
  • Unlock fees, taxes, or “release the funds.” A large balance appears on screen. To withdraw, you must pay a levy, a KYC fee, or anti-money-laundering clearance — in more crypto. That is the second steal. More money does not unlock the first money.
  • Support only lives in the chat with your match. No phone number you looked up yourself. No firm you can verify on a regulator’s site. They interpret every delay as you “not trusting us.”
  • A small withdrawal that works perfectly the week you hesitate. That is the hook, not an audit.
  • Rush and secrecy. Limited slots, “the window closes tonight,” “don’t tell your bank or family, they won’t understand.” Isolation protects the con.
  • No unscripted live video while money talk grows. Clips and frozen “I’m at the office” stills are not a call. AI dating scams cover the faces.

You do not owe them a debate about the chart.

Real partners and real exchanges do not need you to fund a brokerage from WhatsApp. “I do not send crypto to people I have not met” is a complete sentence. If that boundary ends the chat, the chat needed to end.

What to do now

Stop depositing

No “one last” transfer. No unlock fee. No tax to release funds. More coins do not buy the earlier coins back, and they do not make a real broker appear.

Call the bank, card, or exchange same day

Say romance scam or investment fraud. Flag wires, Zelle, and the crypto on-ramp (Coinbase, Cash App, a bank wire into an exchange). Give them the destination wallet and any txid. Speed matters more than perfect wording.

Report the profile, then FTC ReportFraud and IC3

Block and report on the app or social site. US: ReportFraud.ftc.gov and IC3.gov. UK: Action Fraud. Australia: Scamwatch / ReportCyber. Those filings compile wallets and scripts; they are not a refund desk. Checklist: how to report a dating scam.

Save evidence, tell someone you trust

Screenshots of the profile, chat, URLs, wallet addresses, and receipts. Shame is part of the design. A trusted friend reduces the chance you send again under a new “margin call.”

Recovery-scam warning

After a crypto romance loss, a second crew often arrives. They pose as lawyers, blockchain tracers, government agents, or “scam recovery” firms. They say the coins are located. They need a retainer, a tax, a processing fee, or your exchange login “to deposit the refund.”

The FTC’s line is short: do not trust someone who contacts you claiming they can recover lost money for a fee. Government agencies and legitimate organizations will not ask for money or financial details in exchange for a refund. Anyone who does is a scammer. Sometimes it is the same operators in a new costume.

  • They found you. You did not look up the number yourself.
  • They want payment first — retainer, unlock, tax, “AML clearance,” more USDT.
  • They want remote access or your bank / exchange / wallet login.
  • They guarantee a percentage back if you act today.

There is no private firm that can reverse a transfer you already signed, and no official who needs gift cards or more crypto to release a seizure. If a recovery pitch already took more money, treat that as a new scam: stop, call the bank again, file another ReportFraud and IC3 report. Do not hire a third person to fix the second. FAQ

What is a crypto romance scam?

A fake relationship used to get you to send cryptocurrency — a wallet for an emergency, a “test trade,” or a login on a fake exchange. The dating-app or WhatsApp warmth is the setup. Hub: romance scams explained.

Is this the same as pig butchering?

Overlap, not a synonym. Pig butchering is the long version with a fake trading dashboard and a withdrawal hook. A crypto romance scam can also be a shorter wallet ask with no platform. If you have a dashboard they sent you, start here: pig butchering · dating-app angle.

What should I do if I already sent crypto?

Stop depositing. Do not pay an unlock fee. Call the bank or the exchange the same day, report the profile, then file FTC ReportFraud and IC3. Crypto that left the exchange is often gone. Do not pay a tracer. Steps: report a dating scam · recovery limits.

What are the red flags?

Off-app rush, no live video, a platform or wallet you did not find yourself, guaranteed returns, a perfect small withdrawal, then an unlock fee or tax. One strong money flag is enough. Checklist: dating red flags · romance scam checklist.
