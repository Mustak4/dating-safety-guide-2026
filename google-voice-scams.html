Google Voice scams: fake numbers in dating and romance fraud

A calm handbook for VoIP and burner numbers that show up after a dating match — Google Voice, TextNow, and the rest. A local area code is not a hometown. A voice call is not identity. The useful check is live video you start, on the original app.

Date Safely editors Updated Sep 13, 2026 18+

Quick answer

A Google Voice dating scam is romance fraud that uses a VoIP or burner number so the operator looks local and can vanish the line. Google Voice itself is a real product. The abuse is the costume: a city-matching area code, a “call me so you know I’m real,” then an off-app thread and a money ask. Do not treat the digits as identity. Do not send a one-time code. Stay on the dating app until unscripted live video you start. If cash already left: bank the same day, then FTC ReportFraud and IC3. Do not pay anyone who offers to get it back. Number workflow: scam phone numbers. Identity check: how to verify someone online.

Key takeaways

  • A local or “same city” number is easy to rent. It is not a residency check.
  • A voice call, voice note, or “verify me” ring is not proof the photos are that person.
  • VoIP lines get recycled and caller ID gets spoofed. Reverse lookup is a hint, not a verdict.
  • The steal is still gift cards, wires, payment apps, or crypto — usually after they leave the app.

What a Google Voice dating scam is

People search google voice scams, google voice dating scam, and voip dating scam after a Tinder, Bumble, Hinge, or Facebook match handed them a number that “looks American” — or after a reverse-lookup site labeled the line Google Voice, TextNow, or “VoIP.” Those phrases are the same pattern with different search boxes. The product name is costume. The job of the number is to look like a person who lives near you.

Google Voice, Google Fi numbers, TextNow, TextPlus, TextFree, Burner, Skype-in, and other internet numbers are legitimate tools. Ordinary people use them. Romance crews use them because they are cheap, quick to open, easy to drop, and often carry a US or city area code the dating profile already claimed. A fake phone number dating search is usually this: not a made-up string of digits, but a real line that does not belong to the face in the photos.

This page stays on that phone costume — why it appears in the first days, why a call is not a background check, how recycling and spoofing fool lookup sites, and what to do if money already left. It is not a second catalog of every romance script. Ordinary dating still uses a second number after you have done unscripted live video and you still have not been asked for money. Distance is not the crime. The irreversible payment is.

Why VoIP numbers show up early

The first “proof” a crew offers is often a phone number. It arrives the same day as the match, or the morning after a warm thread. “Here’s my real cell.” The area code matches the city on the profile. That is the point. A dating inbox can be reported. A disposable VoIP line cannot be attached to that report the way a long-held mobile can.

  • They look local on purpose. Google Voice lets you pick an area code. So do most burner apps. “I’m in Dallas too” plus a 214 or 972 number is logistics, not geography. Neighbor spoofing — your own area code on caller ID — is a related trick. The digits on the screen are a label. They are not a passport stamp.
  • The line is disposable. If you report the dating profile, they keep the WhatsApp or SMS thread on a new Voice number by Friday. “My old phone died” is the usual line. The person did not move cities. The account did.
  • A “real number” is treated as a character reference. Many people still believe only a scammer would refuse to talk on the phone. Crews know that. They want the call, because a call feels intimate and still hides the face. The number is there so you stop asking for video.
  • The number is the off-app door. Once they have SMS or a Voice-to-Voice call, the dating-app report button stays behind. Same hinge as a WhatsApp rush, different costume. Off-app playbook: WhatsApp dating scam. Facebook-to-Messenger version: Facebook dating scams.

You do not need to become a telecom expert. If the first urgency is “text my number” or “call me so you know I’m real,” treat that as data — the same way you would treat a same-day WhatsApp ask. Stay in the original inbox until live video. Walk-away list: dating scam red flags.

Why a voice call is not identity

A common line is “call me — I’ll verify I’m real.” Sometimes they offer to call you so you can hear “their” voice. That is not verification. It is audio. Photos can be stolen. Voices can be anyone on a payroll, a cloned clip, or a different age and accent than the profile promised. Face-swap tools still fail a sudden turn. A phone call does not ask for a turn.

  • “Verify me” is not a KYC check. Banks and dating apps do not prove a crush by ringing a Google Voice line. If they ask you to read a one-time code that just arrived by SMS — “so I know this is your phone,” “so Google will unlock my Voice,” “so WhatsApp will verify” — that code is the key to your account. Once you say it, the account is theirs. Do not read it back. Number-and-code workflow: scam phone numbers.
  • A voice note is inventory. So is a “good morning” clip. Operators reuse them. Ask for something they could not have filed: a wave across the face, a turn, a nonsense word, the book on the table — on a live call you started. Full checklist: how to verify someone online.
  • They refuse video and offer the phone instead. “Camera is broken.” “No FaceTime on this phone.” “Voice is more personal.” Phones that can run Google Voice can usually run a front camera. A week of audio while money talk grows is a stall, not a shy person. Photo tells: how to spot fake profiles.

If they needed a phone call to prove they are real, they are not done proving it.

A real match can stay on the dating app and do five minutes of unscripted video. A real person can handle “I don’t call unknown numbers” and “I don’t send codes.” If that boundary ends the chat, the chat needed to end.

Recycled and spoofed numbers

Two facts make lookup sites look more certain than they are. First, VoIP and even regular mobiles get reassigned. A Google Voice number you look up today may have belonged to a nurse in Ohio last year. The people-search hit — a name, an old Facebook, a street — can be the previous tenant. Second, caller ID is routinely spoofed. The FCC’s consumer guides treat the name and number on the screen as a label, not a signature. Operators can display a bank, your area code, or the same digits they just texted you.

  • A reverse lookup that “matches their name” is easy to arrange and easy to misread. They picked the name. Or the listing is leftover from the last owner. Or the site sold a comment. It is not a background check.
  • A blank result is the default for a new Voice line. Brand-new VoIP has no forum thread. That is what “clean” looks like. Generated faces fail reverse image search the same way. Blank means unknown. Live video is still the dating check.
  • A “Google Voice” or “VoIP” label is a reason to slow down, not a courtroom verdict. Plenty of honest people use Voice. Plenty of romance scripts do too. Combine it with a same-day off-app rush, refused video, or a money story — that combination is enough to walk away. You do not owe them a debate about a carrier lookup.
  • Spoofing can put “their” mobile on your screen while the call comes from somewhere else. A number that matches a text is not proof the text and the call came from one honest phone. Callbacks can confirm your line is live. Do not call back to “check.” Details: scam phone numbers.

Save the screenshots of what you searched. If you later file with the FTC or IC3, the trail matters more than a paid “full report” from a lookup site.

Off-app move and the money ask

The number is rarely the steal. It is the hallway. After the Voice line feels like “their real life,” the thread leaves Tinder, Bumble, Hinge, or Facebook Dating. Then the first request is late, small, and urgent. Amount is not the test. A $50 “just this once” and a $2,000 “last ticket” are the same crime.

  • Gift cards. iTunes, Apple, Google Play, Steam, Target, Amazon, Vanilla. They may call you from the Voice number and walk you down the aisle. Once you share the code, treat the balance as cash.
  • Wires and payment apps. Western Union, a bank wire, Zelle, Cash App, Apple Pay, PayPal. Hospital deposit, ticket “so I can come see you,” a locked hotel. Someone you have not met on live video should not be on those rails.
  • Crypto or a trading screenshot. A wallet address for a visa, or a “friend’s platform” after weeks of SMS warmth. A small withdrawal that “works” is the hook. Dating-app arc: pig butchering on dating apps.
  • Customs, military, or unlock fees. A held package, leave papers, a “release” to withdraw a fake profit. Real agencies do not collect PINs from a dating match. More coins or another card do not unlock the first loss.

SMS that started as a “wrong number” and turned warm is the same crew, different door. Channel guide: scam text messages. You do not owe them a debate about the hospital or the chart. One strong money ask from someone you have not met is enough to stop.

Practical checks

You are not trying to win an argument in the chat. You are collecting a few facts that either pass or fail. One strong fail is enough.

  • Reverse lookup — cautiously. Search the digits in quotes. If forums say “scam,” walk away. If a people-search site shows a name that matches the profile, do not treat that as a green light. If the result is blank or says VoIP / Google Voice, treat it as unknown. Do not pay for a deeper report. Do not call the number to ask who they are. Phone workflow: scam phone numbers.
  • Live video that you start. Prefer the dating app’s own video if it exists. Do not join a link they pasted from a new domain. Ask for unscripted motion in the moment. A gym clip is not that check. Repeat on a second day if the first call was short and oddly still. How-to: how to verify someone online.
  • Payment red flags. Gift cards, wires, Zelle / Cash App, crypto, an unlock or customs fee, a selfie-plus-ID “so I can trust you.” Nobody who is who they say they are bills you iTunes codes to prove they are real. Isolation — “don’t tell your friends” — protects the rail.
  • Stay on the app until those checks pass. A Voice number, WhatsApp, Telegram, or Messenger hop before video is the usual next step, not a preference. WhatsApp hinge: WhatsApp dating scam.

You do not owe them a debate about Google Voice.

Real people who want a relationship can handle “I stay on the app until live video” and “I do not send money to people I have not met.” Honest Voice users can wait. The ones who punish a no with guilt are running a script.

What to do the same day

Order matters more than a perfect write-up. If you are still holding a gift card, put it back. If a transfer is pending, call the bank before you reread the thread. If they asked for a code, assume that account is burned until you change it from the official app — not from a link in the chat.

Stop sending

No last gift card. No second brand because the first “failed.” No unlock fee. No “one last” call to catch them. No one-time code. More money does not buy the earlier money back. They already know.

Call the bank or card same day

Use the number on the back of the card — not a number from the chat or the Voice line. Say romance scam or romance fraud. Flag wires, Zelle, gift-card purchase points, crypto on-ramps, and any cash handoff. Speed matters more than a long love story.

Tell the carrier, then the platform

On iPhone or Android, mark the caller as junk if the OS offers it. Tell Verizon, AT&T, T-Mobile, or your MVNO the number was used in a dating scam. Forward scam texts to 7726 (SPAM) in the US. Then block and report the dating profile so other people stop matching them. Carriers cannot “trace” a spoofed or Voice ID for you over chat support — they still want the report. Taps: how to report a dating scam.

File FTC ReportFraud and IC3

US: ReportFraud.ftc.gov and IC3.gov. Include the number, the app username, and how you paid if money left. UK: Action Fraud. Australia: Scamwatch / ReportCyber. Those filings compile scripts and wallets; they are not a refund desk. Save screenshots of the profile, the Voice number, the thread, and receipts before you block if you can. Tell one trusted person.

Recovery-scam warning

After a Google Voice or burner-number loss — or after you merely searched the digits — a second crew often arrives. They pose as lawyers, “number tracers,” crypto recoverers, carrier support, or even the FTC. They say the money is located, or they can prove who owned the Voice line. They need a retainer, a tax, a processing fee, or remote access “to deposit the refund.”

The FTC’s line is short: do not trust someone who contacts you claiming they can recover lost money for a fee. Government agencies and legitimate organizations will not ask for money or financial details in exchange for a refund. Anyone who does is a scammer. Sometimes it is the same operators in a new costume.

  • They found you — you did not look up the agency yourself.
  • They want payment first — retainer, unlock, tax, “clearance,” another gift card, or a fee to “trace” the Voice number.
  • They want remote access or a bank / wallet / email / phone login.
  • They tell you not to talk to family or the bank because it would “tip off” the first scammer.

Paying them does not recover the first loss. It funds the second. If a recovery pitch already took more money, treat that as a new scam: stop, call the bank again, file another ReportFraud and IC3 report. Do not hire a third person to fix the second. Full map: get money back from a scam.

FAQ

Are Google Voice numbers a sign of a dating scam?

They are a risk marker, not a verdict. Honest people use Voice. Romance crews use it because it looks local and drops cleanly. Combine a VoIP line with a same-day off-app rush, refused live video, or a money ask — that is enough to stop. Related: scam phone numbers.

Does talking on the phone prove they are who they say they are?

No. A call is audio. Stolen photos and a hired voice (or a cloned clip) still pass a phone test. You start a live video call and ask for motion they could not have filmed. Guide: how to verify someone online.

Why would a scammer use my city’s area code?

So you pick up and relax. Google Voice and other VoIP apps let you choose the area code. Caller ID can also be spoofed. A matching city is the product, not proof they live there.

What if a reverse lookup shows a different name?

That can be the previous owner of a recycled number, a bad listing, or a spoof. It is a reason to stay on the app and demand live video — not a reason to call and confront them. Do not send a code to “clear” the listing.

What should I do if I already sent money after a Google Voice chat?

Stop. Call the bank or card the same day. Report and block the profile. Tell the carrier. File FTC ReportFraud and IC3. Tell one trusted person. Do not pay a recovery firm. Steps: get money back from a scam · how to report.

Should I pay a company that says they can trace the number or get the money back?

No. A retainer, tax, or unlock fee is the second steal. Legitimate agencies never charge for a refund or a “Voice trace.” Anyone who does is a scammer. Detail: recovery-scam warning.
